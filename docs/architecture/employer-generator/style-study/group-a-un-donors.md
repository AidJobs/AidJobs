# Job-advert style guide, group A: UN Secretariat, UN agencies, government donors, development contractors

Desk research, read-only. Compiled 7 November 2026.

## How this was researched, and limits

- I read 12 public adverts, 3 per format, plus one partial extra (a GIZ national post, seen only as a search excerpt). All are from 2025–2026. Seniority runs from G-6 assistant up to team leader, and the functions are programme, finance, M&E, WASH, health/social protection, climate and human capital.
- **Access problems.** The fetch tool got a 403 error from `careers.un.org`, `reliefweb.int` and `unjoblink.org`. For the UN Secretariat, UNICEF, WFP and UNDP adverts I therefore read aggregator mirrors (`unzhaopin.com`, `untalent.org`, and a UNDP mirror captured through search). These copy the official text word for word but can drop tables (such as the UN language-level grid) and header fields (grade, contract type). Where a mirror may have dropped something, I say so. The FCDO, GIZ and Chemonics adverts were read on the employers' own sites. The Palladium and DAI adverts were read on job boards that republish the employer's text.
- **How I measured.** Where I could download the page, I counted words with a simple script that strips the HTML and splits the text at the section headings. Where I could not, I estimated by reading. Every figure is **approximate (±10%)**. The counts leave out the aggregators' own text (for example the "potential interview questions" a mirror added to the UNDP advert).
- **Quoting.** Quoted text is limited to short standard sector phrases (under 15 words), each marked *(std)*. Everything else is my summary.

### (c) Voice and sentence patterns

- **Org. Setting:** third person, formal and declarative. The usual order is where the post sits (unit, then division, then department, then city), then who the incumbent reports to, sometimes followed by one or two sentences on the entity's mandate. Sentences run about 20–35 words. OCHA adverts add a stock paragraph on OCHA's mission of about 70 words.
- **Responsibilities:** the list opens with a fixed lead-in (see the standard phrases). Each bullet starts with a third-person present-tense verb (*Develops, Monitors, Coordinates, Prepares, Liaises, Provides*), and several actions are chained with semicolons. At P-level a bullet runs **about 30–60 words**. At G-level it runs about 8–20 words. The list nearly always ends with a catch-all ("other duties as required").
- **Competencies:** fixed text from the UN competency framework, with each competency's name in capitals followed by its behavioural indicators written as present-tense verb phrases. Only the opening "knowledge" sentence of PROFESSIONALISM is adapted to the job.
- **Requirements:** fixed formula sentences. Each statement is a single sentence ending in "is required" or "is desirable".
- **Boilerplate:** legalistic sentences of 30–50 words with references to administrative instructions (ST/AI/...) and staff rules.
- Spelling is mostly US ("analyze"), mixed in places.

### (d) Section-by-section content rules

1. **Org. Setting and Reporting.** States the unit, division and department, the duty station, the first reporting officer and sometimes a second reporting line. It may include one sentence on what the unit does and a link to the entity's website. TJOs and roster openings name the recruitment type here. **It never covers** team size, budget, or what success looks like.
2. **Responsibilities.** This is the generic job profile for the job family and level, lightly adapted. Entity- or topic-specific clauses are bolted onto generic bullets: in S1, for example, organisational-change wording is added to the standard programme-management bullets. Two different OHCHR P-4 Programme Management Officer adverts shared most of their bullets word for word. The list covers the function's full cycle: plan, monitor, analyse, draft, backstop meetings, outreach, field missions, budget and funding, then the catch-all. **It does not cover** deliverables, KPIs, decision rights or travel percentage. "Leads and/or participates in field missions" is the only hint of travel.
3. **Competencies.** Usually three, taken from Professionalism, Planning and Organizing, Accountability, Teamwork, Communication, Client Orientation and so on, with Managerial competencies added for managers. The wording is fixed.
4. **Education.** P-level: an advanced degree in listed fields "or a related field", with a first degree plus two extra years of experience accepted as a substitute. G-level: a high-school diploma. Desirable certificates may be added.
5. **Work Experience.** The minimum years by grade are fixed (P-2: 2, P-3: 5, P-4: 7; G-6: 7, or 5 with a degree). This is followed by specific required and desirable experience statements, which is where the real selection criteria appear (for example ERP/Umoja, donor reporting, ISO standards).
6. **Languages.** A fixed sentence naming English and French as working languages, then "required/desirable" per language, then a reference to the UN Language Framework level table (mirrors often lose the table).
7. **Assessment.** A one-sentence formula: an assessment exercise, then a competency-based interview.
8. **Special Notice.** Contract duration and funding conditions, gender-parity encouragement, eligibility rules (Young Professionals Programme, internal-candidate limits, retirees, nationality restrictions for field missions), and the recruitment non-discrimination statement. In TJOs this section is the longest in the advert.
9. **United Nations Considerations.** Fixed text: Charter Article 101(3), exclusion for human-rights/humanitarian-law violations and sexual exploitation, abuse or harassment (with definitions), non-smoking, mobility, Inspira instructions, reference checks, probation, and the deadline time (11:59 p.m. New York time).
10. **No Fee.** A fixed all-capitals warning.

**Never present:** salary figures (pay is set by public ICSC scales and is not stated), benefits detail, team size, or a plain-language summary of the job.

### (e) Standard phrases (short, quoted)

- "This position is located in…" *(std)*; "The incumbent reports to…" *(std)*
- "Within delegated authority, the [title] will be responsible for the following duties:" *(std)*
- "Performs other duties as required." *(std)*
- "Advanced university degree (Master's degree or equivalent) in…" *(std)*
- "in combination with two additional years of qualifying work experience" *(std)*
- "A minimum of seven years of progressively responsible experience in…" *(std; the number of years varies by grade)*
- "English and French are the working languages of the United Nations Secretariat." *(std)*
- "For this job opening, English is required. French is desirable." *(std)*
- "Evaluation of qualified candidates may include an assessment exercise…" *(std)*
- "…followed by competency-based interview." *(std)*
- "Shows pride in work and in achievements" *(std competency indicator)*
- "subject to extension of the mandate and/or the availability of the funds" *(std)*
- "committed to achieving 50/50 gender balance in its staff" *(std)*
- "removed at 11:59 p.m. (New York time) on the deadline date" *(std)*

### (f) Common weaknesses observed

- **Boilerplate outweighs content.** Roughly 1,200–1,600 of about 1,900 words are fixed text. The role itself is hard to find.
- **Generic duties.** The generic-profile bullets ("substantive backstopping", "generates survey initiatives") give no sense of the actual portfolio, scale or priorities. The duties of different P-4 posts read almost the same.
- **Jargon and abbreviations without explanation:** TJO, RfR, Umoja, allotment advices, pre-encumbrances, ST/AI references.
- **Key facts are buried.** The contract length (for example "3 months, possibility of extension") sits in Special Notice, sometimes in capitals, rather than near the top. Salary is never stated.
- **Duplication.** In S2 the nationality-restriction paragraph appears twice word for word. Gender and diversity text repeats across Special Notice and UN Considerations.
- **Empty sections are still shown.** "Job-Specific Qualification: Not available."
- **Mismatches between sections.** In S3 the education requirement is a high-school diploma while the experience section demands specialist ERP and grants-system experience. The requirement is fine for G-level, but readers find it confusing.
- **Org. Setting can be uninformative.** S2 names the unit and supervisor but says nothing about what the team does or why the post exists.
- **On the plus side:** the deadline time zone is always stated (New York), and the requirements are precise and checkable.

### (g) Value-add opportunities for an AI writer (respecting the format)

**Fixed (do not alter):** the header fields, the competency texts, the education and experience formulas and minimum years by grade, the language sentence, the Assessment sentence, the UN Considerations, No Fee, and the gender, mobility and eligibility notices. The **substance** of Responsibilities is tied to the classified generic job profile.

**Where clarity can improve without breaking the rules:**
- **Org. Setting:** add one or two sentences on the team's purpose, its size, the main counterparts, and the post's specific focus. This is allowed, and some entities already do it (OCHA's mandate paragraph, the EMS post's "focus of this job" sentence).
- **Responsibilities:** put the post-specific bullets first, and attach a concrete object to generic verbs (which programme, which donor portfolio, which reports). Keep the third-person "-s" verb style, cap a bullet at about 40 words, and split chains of five or more semicolon clauses.
- **Work Experience:** turn vague "related area" lists into clear required and desirable items, and stop the same item appearing under both.
- **Special Notice:** lead with contract type, duration and funding condition in one plain sentence. Remove duplicated paragraphs, and use sentence case instead of all capitals.
- **AidJobs display layer (outside the advert):** a plain-language summary card showing grade, the matching ICSC salary-scale link, contract length, duty-station hardship or family status if public, and the deadline in New York time **and** in the reader's local time. Spell out abbreviations on first use, and flag sections that say "Not available".

### (c) Voice and sentence patterns

- **UNICEF** mixes voices on purpose. Section headings speak to the reader in the second person ("How can you make a difference?", "To qualify as an advocate for every child you will have…"). The role paragraph is third person ("The WASH Specialist reports to…"), and sentences run long, about 30–45 words with long noun lists. Requirements are label-led bullets (*Education:*, *Work Experience:*, *Skills:*, *Language Requirements:*).
- **UNDP** opens the duties with "She/he will have the following…" and then uses **base-form (imperative) verbs**: *Lead, Coordinate, Conduct, Provide, Support, Promote*. Bullets run 20–45 words, grouped under numbered function headings. The Institutional Arrangement section is third person ("will work under the direct supervision of…"). Competencies show level statements of 8–15 words each.
- **WFP** has numbered accountabilities with base-form verbs (*Contribute, Provide, Develop, Research, Engage*), 15–35 words each, and a capability framework in third-person present tense. "Desired experiences" use the present perfect: *Has deepened…, Has taken leadership…*.
- Across agencies the boilerplate is in the agency's own voice ("We offer…", "UNICEF does not hire…").

### (d) Section-by-section content rules

1. **Agency intro.** The mission, an employer-value statement, and sometimes perks (WFP's "Why join"; UNICEF mentions an "attractive compensation and benefits package" but gives no figures).
2. **Background / Context.** UNDP gives country macro data (GDP, poverty, HDI), the office structure and the project description, with the post appearing only at the end. WFP gives the office, the reporting line, and a standard paragraph on the level's autonomy. UNICEF gives the section, the reporting line and the technical scope.
3. **Duties.** Functional groupings. UNDP lists detailed operational tasks with systems and products named (dashboards, ROAR, donor reports). WFP uses generic, level-based accountabilities ("not all-inclusive, within delegated authority"). UNICEF lists only the headline areas and links to a PDF job description.
4. **Institutional arrangement (UNDP).** The supervisor, a technical or dotted reporting line, and who the post supervises. This is the clearest statement of reporting and management scope in any format.
5. **Competencies.** UNDP: the core competencies at a stated level, plus cross-functional and technical competencies (thematic area, name, definition). UNICEF: seven named competencies and the CRITAS values, linked to the framework. WFP: the "4Ps" (Purpose, People, Performance, Partnership) with indicators.
6. **Requirements.** UNDP uses an education OR-formula and a years table by degree (master's plus 2 years, or bachelor's plus 4), then required-experience bullets, a separate "desired skills" list, languages and certificates. UNICEF gives education, years, a developing-country deployment requirement, skills keywords and language. WFP gives education, language at "level C", and years of experience.
7. **Closing.** Diversity and accommodation, the safeguarding and background-check regime (UNICEF's is very long, including the Misconduct Disclosure Scheme), medical clearance, visa, no-fee warning, Article 101 remark, humanitarian deployability (UNICEF), the right to select multiple candidates (UNDP), and guidance on candidates' use of AI (UNDP).

**Usually missing:** salary (none of the three gave a figure), contract length (UNICEF and UNDP mirrors showed only the contract type in the title), deliverables or KPIs, and travel percentage.

### (e) Standard phrases (short, quoted)

- UNICEF: "How can you make a difference?" *(std)*; "To qualify as an advocate for every child you will have…" *(std)*; "For every Child, you demonstrate…" *(std)*; "Master or equivalent (Advanced University Degree) in…" *(std)*; "One year of deployment in a developing country is required." *(std)*
- UNDP: "Institutional Arrangement" *(std heading)*; "will work under the direct supervision of" *(std)*; "will be given due consideration in lieu of the advanced university degree" *(std)*; "performs other duties within their functional profile" *(std)*
- WFP: "KEY ACCOUNTABILITIES (not all-inclusive, within delegated authority)" *(std)*; "Fluency (level C) in English" *(std)*; "Other as required." *(std)*
- Shared: "the paramount consideration in the employment of the staff" *(std, Charter Art. 101)*; "Only shortlisted candidates will be contacted" *(std)*

### (f) Common weaknesses observed

- **Duties hidden in a PDF (UNICEF).** The body gives six headline areas, so search engines and quick readers never see the actual tasks.
- **Context before role (UNDP).** About 600 words of country statistics come before the reader learns what the job is. There are also 35 duty bullets, many of which overlap (for example coordination with government appears in three groups).
- **Contradictions.** WFP lists "at least 5 years of experience is desirable" under *minimum* qualifications. Its "Desired experiences" list repeats two items word for word.
- **Typos and copy-paste errors.** "Desirabel Skills" (UNDP), "A least 5 years" (WFP), and a UNICEF education list that repeats the same fields in different capitalisation.
- **Missing salary,** even where the advert promises an "attractive" package. The WFP text points to a "Terms and Conditions section" that does not appear in the mirror; I cannot confirm whether the original includes it.
- **Abbreviations.** NSSS, ROAR, POPP, RBM, GRS, MIS, CRITAS, CBT and NOA are used without being spelled out.
- **Boilerplate share is 35–60%,** and UNICEF's safeguarding text repeats the background-check sentence twice.
- **Deadline time zones are inconsistent:** WFP states it clearly, the UNICEF and UNDP mirrors show it converted or not at all.

### (g) Value-add opportunities for an AI writer (respecting the format)

**Fixed:** the agency headings and their order, the competency framework names and levels, the education/experience formulas (UNDP's years-by-degree table, UNICEF's "Master or equivalent" line), the safeguarding and medical clauses, and the tier/eligibility notes.

**Flexible, so improve it:**
- **Background:** cap it at about 150–200 words and lead with the project and the post's purpose. Country statistics should get one sentence at most.
- **Duties:** 4–6 function groups with 3–6 bullets each (about 20–30 bullets maximum at analyst level), base-form verbs for UNDP and WFP, no duplicated bullets across groups. For UNICEF, **bring the PDF's key accountabilities into the body** as short bullets under the six headings.
- **Institutional arrangement:** always state the supervisor, any technical line, the number of direct reports, and the main counterparts. Encourage this for UNICEF and WFP too, in their own context paragraphs.
- **Requirements:** separate "required" from "desirable" strictly, check that the years under "minimum" are actually required, and spell out abbreviations on first use.
- **Plain facts block (AidJobs layer):** contract type and length, grade, salary-scale link or local salary range where public, deadline with time zone, nationality eligibility (NO and GS posts are for nationals), deployment and travel expectations.
- **Automated checks:** typos, repeated list items, contradictions between "minimum" and "desirable", and references to sections that do not exist.

### (c) Voice and sentence patterns

- **GIZ:** second-person framing ("We are looking for you", "Your tasks", "Your profile", "Our offer", "You will work as…"). Task bullets **start with gerunds** (*Providing advisory work…, Supporting…, Facilitating…, Offering advice…*) and are long, 20–50 words, often with "(e.g. …)" examples. Profile bullets are noun phrases (*Master of Science in…, At least ten years…, Demonstrated experience in…*). In the D4 excerpt, the national-post variant also used gerunds ("Designing…", "Overseeing…") and ended with a catch-all line about additional tasks requested by management.
- **FCDO:** context paragraphs in the third person ("The postholder will…", "The successful candidate will demonstrate…"), 25–35 words per sentence, with a confident, persuasive tone; D3 adds "This role would suit someone who…". Responsibility bullets use **imperative verbs** (*Act as, Provide, Lead, Build, Promote, Ensure, Work with*), 15–40 words each. Essential and desirable criteria are noun phrases or "Ability to…" or "Experience…" lines that rarely state years.

### (d) Section-by-section content rules

- **GIZ**
  1. Scope summary: what the advisory mandate is and for which counterpart institution.
  2. Tasks: grouped by work stream, **sometimes with time-share percentages** (D1: one stream at 80%, the remaining 20% not described). Policy-advisory wording ("advisory work on the design of…").
  3. Profile: strictly ordered sub-heads (education/training, then professional experience with years, then additional location- and work-related know-how, then personal soft skills). Years of experience are explicit (D1: 10 years plus 5 years in-country).
  4. Offer: generic benefits ("attractive monthly allowances in addition to the local salary"), preparation for the assignment, contract duration stated vaguely.
  5. About CIM/GIZ, then the contact. The closing date has **no time zone**. In D4 (national post), the closing text gives document requirements (CV and letter in English), the email route, a diversity line and the national terms and conditions.
- **FCDO**
  1. Main purpose: context (country challenge, UK partnership), the role's purpose, and its place in the team. It names concrete accountability roles such as **Programme Responsible Owner (PRO)** and frameworks such as **PrOF**.
  2. Roles and responsibilities: programme cycle, financial forecasting, risk tools (due diligence, delivery-chain maps, fraud and safeguarding), policy advice, partnerships, plus "wider contribution to the Mission" (D3).
  3. Essential and desirable criteria: competence statements, with language and local-context items under desirable.
  4. **Behaviours:** 4 names from the Civil Service Success Profiles (for example Communicating and Influencing, Delivering at Pace, Making Effective Decisions, Leadership, Working Together, Managing a Quality Service, Seeing the Big Picture, Changing and Improving).
  5. **Structured facts block:** deadline with local time zone, grade, full-time or permanent, hours per week, **salary in local currency per month**, start date.
  6. Other benefits: **line-management and budget scope** (D3: no direct reports; about £1M a year of programme spend), L&D days.
  7. Fixed additional information: originality and AI-use rule, no fees, local employment law, the candidate's responsibility for work permits, Success Profiles link, security vetting, reserve list for 12 months.

### (e) Standard phrases (short, quoted)

- GIZ: "Your tasks" / "Your profile" / "Our offer" *(std headings)*; "Integrated Expert" *(std role type)*; "Where appropriate and reasonable, performing additional tasks upon request of the management" *(std)*; "GIZ is committed to diversity and gender balance." *(std)*
- FCDO: "Main purpose of job" *(std heading)*; "Essential qualifications, skills and experience" *(std heading)*; "Required behaviours" *(std)*; "Essential on arrival" *(std in older FCDO adverts)*; "Programme Responsible Owner (PRO)" *(std)*; "Programme Operating Framework (PrOF)" *(std)*; "value for money" *(std)*; "Base salary is subject to tax and other statutory deductions" *(std)*; "Reference checking and security clearances will be conducted" *(std)*; "Appointable candidates who were unsuccessful may be placed on a 'reserve list'" *(std)*

### (f) Common weaknesses observed

- **FCDO's long abstract purpose statement.** D2's purpose section has about 400 words of leadership adjectives ("strong strategic judgement", "managing complexity") before the concrete programme is named.
- **Overlapping essential criteria.** D2 lists 16, with analytical skill, partnership-building and influencing each appearing two or three times. "Significant experience" is never quantified.
- **Behaviours given as names only.** The definitions sit on an external link (other FCDO adverts do include one-line definitions).
- **Salary currency can be ambiguous.** D2's salary figure appeared without a currency in our read; this may be a page-rendering issue.
- **GIZ: no salary figure.** Only "attractive allowances". Contract length is vague ("initially for more than one year"). The closing date has no time zone. Percentages are given for only one task stream. The page repeats the whole advert twice (a template quirk).
- **Demanding requirements.** D1 asks for 10 years overall plus 5 years with Cambodian institutions plus an MSc in a narrow field. That may be justified, but it shrinks the pool.
- **Abbreviations:** GS-NSPC, UHC, PRO, PrOF, AMP, HERA, ISF, ODA.

### (g) Value-add opportunities for an AI writer (respecting the format)

**Fixed:** the FCDO structure and facts fields, the behaviour names (from Success Profiles), the additional-information boilerplate, and the essential vs desirable split. For GIZ: the heading set (tasks, profile, offer), the profile sub-head order, and the CIM/GIZ "About us".

**Flexible, so improve it:**
- **FCDO main purpose:** keep it to 150–220 words. Lead with the programme (name, value, partners) and the 2–3 outcomes the postholder owns, then the team context.
- **Essential criteria:** 6–10 distinct, testable items, each mapped to a responsibility. Remove duplicates, and where FCDO practice allows, turn "significant" into a band ("typically 5+ years").
- **Behaviours:** add the one-line Success Profiles definition next to each name. FCDO already does this in some adverts, so it is within the format.
- **GIZ tasks:** make the percentages add up to 100%. Keep gerund bullets to about 30 words each, and move "e.g." lists into a short "key topics" line.
- **GIZ offer:** state the contract length plainly. If allowances cannot be quantified, link to the public CIM allowance information. Add the closing time and time zone (CET/CEST or local).
- **Scope line for both:** direct reports, budget managed, travel share, and security context where relevant. FCDO's "Staff / Expenditure" lines are a good model to reuse.

### (c) Voice and sentence patterns

- **Third person in the overview** ("The Country Lead will serve as…", "S/he will play a pivotal role…", "The Team Lead is responsible for…"). Sentences run 25–40 words.
- **Bullet verb style depends on the firm:**
  - Chemonics uses **third-person present** (*Provides, Ensures, Oversees, Motivates, Serves*), 15–30 words each.
  - Palladium uses **imperative** (*Design, Facilitate, Provide, Serve, Track, Be responsible*), long at 35–70 words, with multi-sentence bullets.
  - DAI **mixes** them in one list (*Support…*, then *Prepares…, Monitors…, Ensures…*), 10–30 words each.
- **Qualifications** are noun phrases ending in "required" or "preferred" ("Master's degree in … required"; "4+ years of relevant experience…"; "Fluency in English required").
- **Vocabulary is commercial and client-focused:** "client", "primary point of contact", "contractual commitments", "value for money", "compliance with [donor] regulations", "LOE", "consortium partners", "subcontractors".

### (d) Section-by-section content rules

1. **Company blurb:** global footprint, years in operation, mission.
2. **Programme background:** donor (FCDO, EBRD, formerly USAID), programme name, objectives, phase and duration. This is where the **funding or award status** sits ("anticipated proposal", "contingent upon award", "annual contract review based on availability of project funding").
3. **Logistics line (a strength):** location, reporting line, **remuneration basis** (usually "negotiable" or "market rates", rarely a figure), **level of effort (LOE)** and time frame (C3: up to 220 days a year, Jan 2026 to Mar 2028), part-time or full-time, start date if the award is won.
4. **Position overview:** the role's accountability in one paragraph (primary representative, relationship owner, financial responsibility, reporting).
5. **Responsibilities:** grouped by function (Leadership; Client and stakeholder relations; Work planning and adaptive management; MEL; Financial management; Reporting). They carry **contract-delivery content**: workplans, logframe milestones, AMELP, trackers, TORs, forecasts, VfM, risk escalation, donor reporting systems.
6. **Qualifications:** degree, minimum years (stated as "X+" or "Minimum X years"), donor-specific experience (FCDO or USAID rules, the specific country), technical skills (Excel, Oracle), languages.
7. **Competencies (some firms):** short labelled competencies ("Strategic leadership: …", "Financial acumen: …").
8. **How to apply:** an email or ATS link, the subject-line format, a deadline (sometimes absent), "only shortlisted candidates will be contacted", sometimes a feedback timeline (C1: within 30 days).
9. **Safeguarding:** survivor-centred policy, DBS or police checks, and the Inter-Agency Misconduct Disclosure Scheme.

### (e) Standard phrases (short, quoted)

- "this position is for an anticipated proposal" *(std)*
- "contingent upon award of a contract to [firm]" *(std)*
- "subject to USAID approval and funding availability" *(std, pre-2025 US-funded adverts)*
- "annual contract review based on availability of project funding" *(std)*
- "Fees are subject to negotiation in line with market rates" *(std)*
- "primary point of contact with the client" *(std)*
- "ensuring compliance with [donor] regulations" *(std)*
- "Inter-Agency Misconduct Disclosure Scheme" *(std)*
- "Only shortlisted candidates will be contacted." *(std)*
- "Perform additional duties as assigned." *(std)*

### (f) Common weaknesses observed

- **No salary figure** in any of the three ("negotiable", "market rates", or nothing).
- **Closing dates missing or inconsistent.** C1 gives none. C2's board shows "not specified". C3's text and board disagree by one day. No time zones anywhere.
- **Template errors.** C1 numbers two sections "2)" with no "1)", and switches between "TL" and "CL" for the same role. C3 has an orphan "Key tasks will include:" inside the first bullet, and mixes verb forms.
- **Dense abbreviations:** AWP, TA, TOR, CL, P&S, DT, SRHR, AMELP, LOE, PMU, PFI, VfM, often with no first-use expansion.
- **Proposal-stage roles** (C1, Palladium CASA 3) are not clearly separated from live jobs in titles or on job boards. The contingency sentence is buried mid-advert.
- **Part-time roles without LOE.** C1 says part-time 2026–2027 but gives no days.
- **Long multi-sentence bullets** (Palladium) that pack three duties into one.
- **High experience bars** (C1: 15+ years plus published writing) for part-time roles.

### (g) Value-add opportunities for an AI writer (respecting the format)

**Fixed:** the funding or award contingency language (legally required, so keep it verbatim), the client/donor compliance references, the safeguarding and MDS clauses, and the firm's house headings.

**Flexible, so improve it:**
- **Title and top line:** tag the status clearly, e.g. "(Proposal – contingent on award)" or "(Live position)". Put a facts block at the top: donor, programme, location/remote, full- or part-time with **LOE days**, duration, reporting line, remuneration basis or range, closing date **with time zone**.
- **Responsibilities:** pick **one** verb style per firm (Chemonics "-s" verbs, Palladium imperative), use 4–6 groups with 3–5 bullets each, one duty per bullet, about 30 words maximum.
- **Deliverables / KPIs:** add a short "What success looks like in year 1" or "Key deliverables" list drawn from the bullets (workplans, logframe milestones, reports, audits). This is natural for contractors, and the format allows it.
- **Expand abbreviations on first use,** and make numbering and role names consistent.
- **Qualifications:** split required from preferred, quantify experience, and match each item to a duty.
