# Group B: international NGO advert formats, style guide for an AI job-description writer

Desk research, 7 November 2026. Read-only; nothing was submitted, signed up for or logged into.

## How this was done, and its limits

- 15 adverts were read in full or nearly in full (12 required, plus 3 extra for context), all but two dated 2026. One more (Christian Aid South Sudan, older) was seen only as a search snippet and is not counted.
- **Reading problems.** The fetch tool got a 404 from every ReliefWeb job page it tried, a 403 from Oxfam GB's own careers site (`jobs.oxfam.org.uk`), and an empty page from World Vision's Workday site. In those cases I read the same advert from a mirror (Impactpool, UNjobs, UNTalent, CharityJob, ngojobsinafrica, worldngojobs, Ajiriwa, unvacancies, Jobs in Brussels) or from the long text extract the search engine returned. Each source below says which. Mirrors sometimes drop or reformat the employer's standard closing blocks, so boilerplate counts for mirrored adverts may be low.
- **Measurements are approximate.** Word counts are my estimates from the text I read (roughly ±15%). They cover the advert text only, not site menus, cookie notices or the aggregator's own notes. Bullet counts are exact where I had the full list and marked "~" where I didn't.
- **No advert text is copied** beyond short standard sector phrases (under 15 words, quoted, and marked as standard phrases). Everything else is my own summary.
- **Sample size.** About three adverts per format is enough to show house templates, because these organisations reuse templates heavily. It is not enough for statistics. Treat the ranges as indicative only.

### (b) Length

- **Typical total: about 1,600 to 2,400 words for SCI and about 500 to 900 for World Vision country-office posts.** Range in this sample: about 520 (1C) to about 2,400 (1B).
- **Per section (SCI, 1A / 1B):**
  - Header and organisation block (the three "breakthroughs" for children, the safeguarding preamble): about 170 / 90 words.
  - Role purpose: about 45 / 260 words. SCI's length varies a lot; 1B opens with the country office's history.
  - Scope of role: about 40 / 130 words.
  - Key areas of accountability: about 900 words in ~45 bullets / about 1,150 words in ~45 bullets under 7 subheadings.
  - Behaviours (values in practice): about 170 to 190 words in 11 bullets. The text is **identical** in both adverts.
  - Qualifications, experience and skills: about 250 words in ~17 bullets / about 360 words in ~22 bullets.
  - Closing blocks (equal opportunity, safeguarding, health and safety, application): about 90 / 110 words.
- **World Vision (1C):** organisation and safeguarding preamble about 70; job purpose about 95; major responsibilities 13 bullets, about 180; safeguarding 2 bullets, about 35; qualifications about 85; how to apply about 25.

### (c) Voice and sentence patterns

- **Third person throughout.** The text says "the post holder", "the MEAL Manager", "s/he", "his/her"; never "you".
- **Role purpose** is one long paragraph of 25 to 45-word sentences stacked with noun phrases. In SCI's case it often mixes organisation history, scope and team size.
- **Accountability bullets** start with a bare verb ("Review…", "Ensure…", "Lead…", "Support…", "Coordinate…") or with "Responsible for…". In 1A about a third of the bullets start "Responsible for/to". Bullets run 12 to 40 words. Some are really person-specification lines placed among the duties (for example an ability to coach or to find weaknesses).
- **Behaviours** are lowercase fragments with no subject, written as observed traits ("holds…", "sets…", "approachable, good listener").
- **Qualifications** are noun-phrase bullets ("Undergraduate degree in…", "At least 4+ years…", "Experience of…", "Ability to…").
- World Vision uses lettered or numbered sub-items, sometimes mixed with bullets in the same advert, and adds "– Essential" to the end of each line.

### (d) Section-by-section content rules

1. **Summary/header** (SCI's careers site): posted date, closing date **with time and UTC**, country, job family, contract type, hours. Then the team/programme, location, grade (for example "GRADE: 2") and contract length. **No salary figure.**
2. **Child safeguarding level** (SCI): a level from 1 to 3 with a standard definition of how much contact with children the post involves. For a role profile this field is required.
3. **Role purpose**: why the job exists, who it reports to, and which strategy it serves. SCI ties it to its global goals for children. Usually 1 paragraph (50 to 250 words).
4. **Scope of role**: a fixed set of labelled lines: *Reports to*, *Staff reporting to this post* (direct/indirect), *Budget responsibilities*, *Role dimensions* (stakeholders; sometimes the size of the country office, for example annual budget and staff numbers in 1A), and *Context* (humanitarian/development). **This is the format's strongest feature** and it should always be filled.
5. **Key areas of accountability**: 5 to 8 subheadings (for example financial accounting; systems and data quality; banks and cash; staff development; reporting; representation; child safeguarding), each with 3 to 15 verb-led bullets. Sometimes it names real deliverables or KPIs (1A names a month-end cash ceiling and working-day deadlines for the month-end close). Usually it doesn't.
6. **Behaviours (Values in Practice)**: the five SCI values (Accountability, Ambition, Collaboration, Creativity, Integrity) with 1 to 3 standard bullets each. 1B adds a commitment to diversity and anti-racism.
7. **Qualifications / Experience and skills**: degree, years of experience, sector or system knowledge, languages, and readiness to work in emergencies. Essential and desirable items are rarely split clearly. SCI writes "preferred" or "an asset" inline.
8. **Key competencies** (some SCI adverts): technical plus generic competencies with short labels.
9. **Closing blocks**: duties not exhaustive; equality policies; background checks; anti-harassment; health and safety. The application note asks for a CV, a cover letter and, in 1A, the candidate's **current pay and salary expectation**.
10. World Vision variant: *Job purpose*, then *Major responsibilities* (optionally with % of time per area, seen in another World Vision advert), then a standard *Safeguarding* block, then *Knowledge/Qualifications for the Role*, split into required professional experience, required education/training/licence, and preferred knowledge.

### (e) Standard phrases (short, quoted, standard sector phrasing)

- "reflects our commitment to the protection of children from abuse" (SCI safeguarding, standard phrase)
- "rigorous background checks" (standard phrase)
- "BEHAVIOURS (Values in Practice)" (SCI section heading)
- "holds self accountable for making decisions, managing resources efficiently" (SCI behaviour, standard phrase)
- "Willing to take disciplined risks" (SCI behaviour, standard phrase)
- "Save the Children is an equal opportunity employer" (standard phrase)
- "Women are strongly encouraged to apply" (standard phrase, used in country offices)
- "are not exhaustive" (duties disclaimer, standard phrase)
- "we do not employ people whose background is not suitable for working with children" (World Vision, standard phrase)
- "Any additional tasks required by the line manager" (World Vision, standard phrase)

### (f) Common weaknesses seen

- **Leftover template text.** 1B still contains the instruction "(select only one)" beside the safeguarding level. It also ends with internal job-description metadata (who wrote it, a 2023 date, an "Updated by" line and blank "Evaluated" fields). This shows the internal job description was pasted in unchanged.
- **Boilerplate copied across roles.** The behaviours section is word-for-word the same in the finance and MEAL adverts, and says nothing about either job.
- **No salary.** SCI gives only a grade. 1A asks the candidate for their salary expectation while giving none itself. World Vision says the salary follows another agency's scale, and misspells that agency's name.
- **Too long for the reader.** About 45 accountability bullets in 1A, many of them system steps (report codes, working-day deadlines) that matter after hiring, not when applying.
- **Duties and person specification mixed together.** 1A lists "ability" lines as accountabilities.
- **Acronyms and internal names are not explained.** PRIME, REALM, NBD, TAs, SOF, DRC (here a cost code, not the Danish Refugee Council), ER/CAM and TSR01 are used without explanation.
- **Contradictory labels.** World Vision's "Preferred knowledge" list marks most items "– Essential".
- **Generic soft skills** ("ability to work under pressure", "computer literate") take up much of World Vision's short qualifications section.
- **Language quality** in some SCI country-office posts (for example "high complex country", "his/her capacity").

### (g) Value-add opportunities for an AI writer (keeping the role-profile voice)

- **Keep the third-person, formal register** and the section names (Role purpose; Scope of role; Key areas of accountability; Behaviours; Qualifications). Candidates in this sector recognise them.
- **Make "Scope of role" a required fact box.** It should cover reports to; direct and indirect reports (with numbers); budget held (an amount, or "none"); location and field-travel percentage; contract length; grade **and** salary band; safeguarding level. If something is missing, ask the employer instead of leaving it blank.
- **Limit accountabilities to 5 to 7 headed groups of 3 to 6 bullets each (about 25 to 35 bullets).** Move system-level steps into a short "day-to-day systems you will use" line. Keep any measurable target, such as a KPI or deadline, because these are the most useful lines in the advert.
- **Tailor the behaviours.** Keep the five value names, but write one role-specific line under each (for example Integrity for a finance post: preventing fraud in cash handling). The standard text can go behind a link.
- **Write a two-sentence role purpose that leads with the outcome** ("This role exists so that…"). Move country-office history into the organisation block.
- **Find and strip leftover template text** (instructions in brackets, blank fields, the job description's internal approval lines).
- **Expand acronyms on first use and flag internal system names.**
- **Mark each requirement essential or desirable**, check the years of experience asked for against the grade, and flag contradictions.

### (b) Length

- **Typical total: about 1,100 to 2,100 words.** Range: about 1,100 (2B and 2C) to about 2,000 (2A).
- **Per section (2A / 2B / 2C):**
  - Header field list (location, workplace type, hours, salary wording, job family, division, grade, job type, closing date): about 50 / 90 / 40 words.
  - Team purpose: about 105 / none / 30 words. The Oxford HQ and Oxfam International adverts may use "The Role" instead.
  - Job purpose: about 240 / 190 ("The Role") / 30.
  - Dimensions of the role (2C only, a standard grade descriptor): about 80 words, 6 bullets.
  - Key responsibilities: about 1,150 words, ~40 bullets under 7 subheadings / **none in the advert** (they are in the attached job profile) / about 200 words, 9 bullets.
  - What we are looking for: about 250 (partial) / 170 (prose) / 280 words with ~18 bullets in essential, attributes and desirable groups.
  - We offer / Why join us / Flexfam: none seen in 2A / about 120 / about 45.
  - Safeguarding, Misconduct Disclosure Scheme and diversity: about 150 / 170 / 220.
  - How to apply: about 35 / 55 / 30.
  - About us / About Oxfam: none in the 2A mirror / about 320 / about 200.

### (c) Voice and sentence patterns

- **Purpose statements are third person and start with an infinitive or "The [job title] is responsible for…".** Team purpose often begins "To…".
- **"What we are looking for" switches to the first person plural and the second person.** It is the most recognisable sentence in Oxfam adverts. 2B is all second person ("You will bring…", "You will be…").
- **Responsibility bullets are imperative** ("Lead…", "Ensure…", "Contribute to…", "Supervise…", "Represent…", "Promote…"). They are 15 to 35 words each and loaded with abstract nouns (quality assurance, adaptive management, capacity strengthening).
- Feminist-leadership vocabulary appears throughout: a named feminist-leadership approach, practices chosen for assessment, and power, gender justice and inclusion.
- The closing blocks are long standard paragraphs with 25 to 40-word sentences.

### (d) Section-by-section content rules

1. **Header fields** (Oxfam GB 2026 template): location (country, city, hub), workplace type (on-site/hybrid), hours per week, salary wording, job family, division, grade (for example National D1), job type, closing date, country. HQ adverts give an actual salary and a discretionary range. Country-office adverts say the salary follows the Oxfam scale.
2. **(Department purpose) / Team purpose**: 1 to 3 sentences on what the team exists to change, with geography (for example the governorates covered).
3. **Job purpose**: 1 paragraph on the role's leadership/management remit, how it delivers (directly or through partners), and the standards it must follow.
4. **Reporting lines**: in the older template these are separate fields (post holder reports to; jobs reporting to this post; budget responsibility yes/no). In the 2026 mirrors they are often missing.
5. **Dimensions of the role** (older grade-based template): a standard description of autonomy and impact at that grade.
6. **Key responsibilities**: themed subheadings (assessment and evidence; programme development; implementation; monitoring, reporting and learning; representation; partnership management) with imperative bullets. Oxfam International often leaves these out of the advert and refers to the attached job profile PDF instead.
7. **What we are looking for**: starts with the standard sentence on caring about the mission and sharing the feminist values. Then the 2 to 3 feminist-leadership practices chosen for assessment. Then the "ideal candidate will also be / have" list: degree, years, technical areas, languages. A degree-equivalence route is sometimes offered (2A accepts more years of experience instead of a degree).
8. **We offer / Why join us**: HQ adverts list pension, leave, sick pay, life assurance and learning. Oxfam International gives a work-week length and a benefits icon list. Country-office mirrors often have nothing.
9. **Flexfam** (flexible working statement) and a **travel** note.
10. **How to apply**: CV plus a cover letter that addresses the essential criteria in the job profile. Sometimes says only shortlisted candidates will be contacted, with a time frame (2B: about two weeks).
11. **Our values and commitment to safeguarding**: the standard unwanted-behaviour paragraph, screening checks (criminal records, terrorism finance), the Misconduct Disclosure Scheme, and the confirmation that applying means accepting these procedures. Then a diversity sentence.
12. **About us**: confederation size, number of countries, values.

### (e) Standard phrases (short, quoted, standard sector phrasing)

- "cares about Oxfam's mission to end poverty" (standard phrase)
- "feminist principles and values of empowerment, accountability and inclusion" (standard phrase)
- "An ideal candidate for the role will also be / have:" (standard phrase)
- "We believe flexible working is key to building the Oxfam of the future" (Flexfam, standard phrase)
- "explaining your suitability against the essential criteria in the job profile" (standard phrase)
- "preventing any type of unwanted behaviour at work" (standard phrase)
- "participates in the Inter Agency Misconduct Disclosure Scheme" (sector standard)
- "By submitting an application, the job applicant confirms his/her understanding" (sector standard)
- "criminal records and terrorism finance checks" (standard phrase)
- "Safer recruitment" (block label, standard phrase)

### (f) Common weaknesses seen

- **Leftover template placeholders.** 2A begins with an unfilled affiliate placeholder in curly braces. 2B shows "{x} days at {xx:xx} BST" in its header.
- **Title and text disagree.** 2A is advertised as an *Officer* but the job purpose calls the post holder a *Team Leader* in two places.
- **The responsibilities are not in the advert at all** (2B). The candidate has to open a PDF to learn what the job involves.
- **No salary figure** in country-office and Oxfam International adverts. "In line with Oxfam pay range" tells the reader nothing. 2B's "What we offer" paragraph is mostly motivational text.
- **The job purpose is overloaded.** 2A's job purpose runs to about 240 words and repeats the team purpose.
- **Boilerplate outweighs the role.** In 2B the organisation and safeguarding blocks (about 570 words) are longer than the role description and requirements together (about 360).
- **Very long working week** (2A: 48 hours) stated without comment on leave or compensation.
- **Jargon-heavy responsibilities**: IPA, CBOs, SOPs, burn rates, safe programming, GEDSI.
- **Inconsistent closing-date time zones.** 2B gives three time zones at once. Some country-office posts give no time.

### (g) Value-add opportunities for an AI writer (keeping the purpose-led voice)

- **Keep the three-layer structure** (Team purpose, then Job purpose, then Key responsibilities). It is Oxfam's signature. Enforce brevity: team purpose of 1 to 2 sentences (≤40 words), and a job purpose of ≤80 words that doesn't repeat the team purpose.
- **Keep the standard opening line of "What we are looking for" verbatim** (it's house style), then split cleanly into *Essential* and *Desirable*, with the feminist-leadership practices chosen for assessment named and explained in one line each.
- **Always put 6 to 12 key responsibilities in the advert**, even when a full job profile is attached.
- **Pull the decision facts into a block near the top**: reporting line, team supervised, budget holder yes/no and amount, partner portfolio size, field-travel percentage, hours, and the salary band or at least the grade's local range.
- **Check the template**: unfilled braces, title versus body (Officer versus Team Leader), and a single closing time zone.
- **Show the standard safeguarding and Misconduct Disclosure Scheme paragraphs in a consistent collapsed block** on AidJobs, so the role text comes first but nothing legally required is lost.
- **Make "We offer" concrete** (leave days, pension, learning budget, flexible-working options) where the employer supplies them, and ask when it is empty.

### (b) Length

- **Typical total: about 950 to 1,500 words.** Range: about 950 (3A, 3B, 3E) to about 1,500 (3C).
- **Per section:**
  - The Organisation / About us / About CARE: about 110 words (Plan, identical in every advert); about 140 (Christian Aid); about 90 (CARE).
  - The Opportunity / Why work for us: about 60 (3A), 90 (3B), 200 (3C), 140 (3E).
  - The Role / Role purpose: about 110 (3C), 190 (3D), 200 (3E). Plan's officer posts merge this into "The Opportunity".
  - Key responsibilities / "In this role, you will": 10 numbered items, about 330 words (3A); 15 bullets, about 430 (3B); 13 bullets, about 330 (3C); **none** in 3D and 3E (prose only).
  - About you: 10 bullets, about 150 (3A); 17 bullets, about 280 (3B); 18 bullets, about 300 (3C); 14 bullets in essential and desirable groups, about 275 (3D); one prose paragraph, about 150 (3E).
  - Practical facts (location, type of role, reports to, grade, salary, closing date): about 25 to 40 words. 3C has about 190 because of a long list of eligible countries and a salary disclaimer.
  - Equality, safeguarding and Misconduct Disclosure Scheme boilerplate: about 260 (Plan), 280 (Christian Aid), 365 (CARE, including the right-to-work paragraph).

### (c) Voice and sentence patterns

- **Second person and conversational.** "As [title], you will…", "You will be a relationship builder…", "You are an experienced…", "To be successful in this role, you will have:". The organisation block uses "we".
- The opportunity paragraph typically uses **"This is an exciting / compelling / unique opportunity for…"** followed by one motivational sentence about impact.
- Responsibility bullets are still **imperative and verb-led** ("Provide…", "Support…", "Champion…", "Lead…", "Ensure…"). The "you" lives in the prose around them.
- "About you" bullets are noun phrases ("A Bachelor's degree…", "At least 4 years'…", "Strong experience in…", "Excellent…"), sometimes introduced by a "you" sentence.
- CARE UK and Christian Aid managers' adverts are **almost entirely prose**: 3 to 5 paragraphs of 20 to 35-word sentences, with no responsibilities list.
- Plan's organisation paragraph ends with a short campaign-style line, and Christian Aid's with a values rallying line. These adverts read the most like a brand voice.

### (d) Section-by-section content rules

1. **The Organisation / About us**: a fixed brand paragraph. Plan's covers girls' rights, 80+ countries and 85+ years. CARE's covers women and girls at the centre, the year founded and annual reach. Christian Aid's covers its movement and churches, the open-to-all-faiths line and flexible working.
2. **The Opportunity**: why the role matters now and where it sits (team, hub, country office). For managers it may include context such as funding shifts, donor mix or a regional focus.
3. **The Role / Role purpose** (where it is separate): what the post holder is responsible for, where they are based, travel, and the main stakeholders.
4. **Key responsibilities / In this role you will**: 8 to 15 imperative bullets, often ungrouped. Field-specific facts sometimes appear here (3B names the governorates to travel to and occasional overnight stays).
5. **About you**: degree, years, technical areas, local law or context knowledge, systems, languages, travel readiness. Plan puts essential and desirable inline ("is desirable", "an advantage"). Christian Aid uses explicit Essential and Desirable headings.
6. **Practical facts at the end** (Plan): Location; Type of role (full-time, fixed-term, nationals only); Reports to; Grade; Closing date, sometimes "rolling". CARE UK puts **salary, contract type, closing date and time, and interview dates at the top**, which is the best practice seen in this group.
7. **Application instruction**: Plan asks candidates to answer the role's requirements in the cover letter and accepts English only. Christian Aid warns the advert may close early.
8. **Equality and safeguarding**: Plan lists the protected characteristics, its safeguarding policy and the Misconduct Disclosure Scheme, and adds an anti-scam line. CARE UK covers zero tolerance, Bridger and criminal checks, the Misconduct Disclosure Scheme, its Disability Confident guaranteed interview, and the groups it particularly welcomes. Christian Aid names under-represented groups it encourages, its faith-identity expectation, DBS/police and counter-terrorism checks, and right to work.

### (e) Standard phrases (short, quoted, standard sector phrasing)

- "We won't stop until we are all equal." (Plan brand line)
- "Please respond to the requirements of the role in your cover letter." (Plan, standard phrase)
- "will never send unsolicited emails requesting payment from candidates" (Plan anti-scam, standard phrase)
- "Equality, diversity and inclusion is at the very heart of" (Plan, standard phrase)
- "This is an exciting opportunity for" (common opener, standard phrase)
- "To be successful in this role, you will have:" (standard phrase)
- "you don't have to be Christian to work here" (Christian Aid, standard phrase)
- "people of all faiths and none" (Christian Aid, standard phrase)
- "zero-tolerance approach" (CARE UK, standard phrase)
- "Disability Confident Employer" (UK scheme, standard phrase)
- "We guarantee interviews to disabled applicants who meet the essential criteria" (CARE UK, standard phrase)
- "Inter Agency Misconduct Disclosure Scheme" (sector standard)

### (f) Common weaknesses seen

- **No responsibilities at all** in the two UK-based employers' manager adverts (3D, 3E). The reader learns the remit only from prose, which is hard to compare across jobs.
- **Salary hidden** at Plan ("will be happy to disclose… set according to your location" in 3C) and Christian Aid ("Competitive"). CARE UK is the clear exception.
- **Boilerplate that doesn't fit the post.** 3D is a Freetown post with a right-to-live-and-work requirement in Sierra Leone, yet the standard text says a UK right-to-work check will be run.
- **Duplicates.** 3D lists managing UN-funded projects twice in the essential criteria. The Mozambique advert (extra) repeats two whole responsibility bullets word for word.
- **Unclear seniority and over-long "About you" lists.** 3C's 18 bullets are almost all "strong / excellent / proven" skills with no years given.
- **Rolling or vague closing dates** (3B "Open on rolling basis"; 3C closes "12th November" with no year or time zone). The closing date is often at the bottom, after the responsibilities.
- **Promotional filler** ("exciting", "unique", "compelling", "dynamic") that doesn't help the candidate.
- **Reporting line missing or inconsistent.** 3B says it reports to an Integrated Protection Programme Manager in the facts block, but the body says the Protection Manager supervises.

### (g) Value-add opportunities for an AI writer (keeping the narrative voice)

- **Keep the warm second person and the brand paragraph**, but cap the organisation block at about 80 words on AidJobs, with "read more" for the full text.
- **Always add a 6 to 10 bullet "What you'll do" list**, even when the employer writes prose. Generate it from the role-purpose paragraphs and keep the employer's verbs.
- **Move the practical facts to the top** (CARE UK's pattern): salary or band, contract length, location and travel, reports to, grade, closing date with time and time zone, interview dates.
- **Split "About you" into Essential (≤8) and Desirable (≤5)**, give years of experience where the grade implies it, and merge duplicates.
- **Check the boilerplate against the post** (UK right-to-work text on a non-UK post; reporting line in the facts block versus the body).
- **Reduce adjectives**: allow one "why this role matters" sentence and cut stacked praise words.
- **Keep the existing inclusion statements** (they are a strength). Where an employer's voice allows, add the gentle line "If you meet most but not all of the essential criteria, we still encourage you to apply". None of the adverts read included this line; Christian Aid's welcome to people from outside the voluntary sector comes closest.

## Format 4: Humanitarian field profile (MSF, IRC, NRC, Mercy Corps)

### (a) Sources read

### (b) Length

- **Typical total: about 1,100 to 2,300 words.** Range: about 750 (4C, without NRC's standard blocks) to about 2,300 (4A and 4B). The MSF pool profile is about 1,500 words, two-thirds of it a technical-competency grid.
- **Per section:**
  - Organisation and context: about 385 (4A: Mercy Corps plus a long INGO Forum and Sudan context), about 260 (4B: IRC, IRC Yemen and the region), about 70 (4D).
  - Main purpose / position summary / scope of work: about 280 (4A), 160 plus an 80-word summary (4B), about 20 (4C, one sentence), about 50 (4D).
  - Accountabilities / main responsibilities: about 620 words, ~28 bullets in 6 groups (4A); about 1,100 words, ~40 bullets in 8 groups (4B); 22 bullets split into generic and specific (4C); 13 core plus 11 context-specific bullets, about 520 words (4D).
  - Key working relationships: about 35 (4A), 50 (4B). 4D gives only a reports-to line; 4C names its managers inside the bullets.
  - Requirements: about 290 words with 10 essential, 4 desirable and 8 "success factor" bullets (4A); about 300 in 4 lettered groups, education, experience, skills and proficiencies (4B); about 280 in NRC's two-part competency model, with 16 professional bullets plus 5 behavioural (4C); about 90 (4D).
  - Conditions (living conditions, working environment, hardship, R&R): about 45 (4A), one sentence in the scope of work (4B), one line on local priority with no housing or transport (4D).
  - Standards, safeguarding, equal employment opportunity and data protection: about 350 (4A), 400 (4B, with one paragraph repeated), 250 (4D).

### (c) Voice and sentence patterns

- **Third person, operational and procedural.** IRC: "The SOC will…", with bullets in the third-person present ("Supports…", "Ensures…", "Leads…"). MSF: gerunds ("Being responsible for…", "Defining and implementing…", "Ensuring…") or bare verbs ("Coordinate…", "Supervise…"). NRC: bare verbs ("Ensure…", "Prepare…", "Represent NRC…").
- **MSF main-purpose sentences are long** (40 to 60 words). They chain verbs and ends ("…in order to ensure…", "…with the aim of…").
- Words like security, compliance and standards appear in almost every bullet group. Each advert has explicit **security** and **safeguarding** groups.
- The requirements in this format are the most **quantified**: years of experience overall and in leadership, languages, and context experience (named countries or regions).
- The conditions text is short and factual (unaccompanied, group housing, utilities with cuts, travel percentage, insecure locations).

### (d) Section-by-section content rules

1. **Header**: location (base and roving share), status, salary wording ("refer to the scale"), and a **safeguarding risk level** with definitions (4A). MSF gives career level, family, hierarchical and functional reporting (pool profile).
2. **Organisation and context**: humanitarian mandate (MSF: neutrality and impartiality) and a crisis narrative (scale, access constraints, who controls which areas).
3. **Main purpose / general position summary / scope of work**: what the role delivers, whom it supports, the senior-management-team membership, and a one-line **duty-station statement** (IRC: unaccompanied, based in Aden, extensive travel).
4. **Accountabilities**: grouped by function (coordination and representation; information; supply chain; IT; data; programme support; team management; partnership; security; safeguarding; accountability to affected people). MSF adds "section/context-specific accountabilities" for the local project, such as co-management with the Ministry of Health. NRC splits generic from specific.
5. **Key working relationships**: reports to; directly supervises (titles); internal contacts; external contacts (clusters, authorities, NGO forums).
6. **Requirements**: education; years of experience, with a leadership sub-requirement; context experience; languages; software. NRC uses its two-part **competency model** (professional, then behavioural; the behavioural ones are standard labels). Mercy Corps adds "success factors", soft traits including temperament.
7. **Conditions**: whether the post is unaccompanied; hardship allowance; R&R; home leave; housing type; utilities; travel share; insecurity. IRC and Mercy Corps include these in some posts but not others. National posts state what is **not** provided (4D: no accommodation or transport; local residents prioritised).
8. **Application and selection** (strongest in MSF national adverts): required documents (motivation letter, CV, ID, certificates, licences), email subject line, hand-delivery addresses, deadline with time, a "no reply within six weeks" rule, data-protection notice, and a free-of-charge statement.
9. **Standards and safeguarding**: IRC's code of conduct (named standards), Mercy Corps' commitment to the UN Secretary-General and IASC sexual exploitation and abuse principles, the Misconduct Disclosure Scheme, an integrity hotline, and gender-equality benefits.

- "dedicate 5% of their time to learning activities" (Mercy Corps, standard phrase)

### (f) Common weaknesses seen

- **Leftover template text.** 4A shows an unticked/ticked risk-level checkbox line and an empty "Supervisory Responsibility" heading. 4B contains a stray "undefined" bullet, a pair of backticks and the same professional-standards paragraph twice. 4C keeps the instruction "(shall be adapted to the specific position)", a bracketed list of placeholder locations, and "(5 years )".
- **Title and text disagree.** 4C's title is *ICLA and Protection Coordinator* but its purpose describes a *Protection from Violence Coordinator*. 4A's header location (Gadaref) differs from its body (Port Sudan, roving).
- **Context crowds out the role.** In 4A the forum and crisis background (about 385 words) comes before the role summary. In 4B the organisation history runs about 260 words.
- **Conditions are thin or missing.** The R&R cycle, hardship classification and housing are often not stated by the employer. The unvacancies mirror added R&R and hardship details from its own metadata, not from IRC's text. 4A gives no R&R terms even though the role covers for others' R&R.
- **No salary** anywhere except as "per scale". There's no closing date in 4A's body text.
- **Requirements that may be unrealistic** for the pay: 4A asks for 10+ years with 4 in leadership. MSF's pool profile requires mastery of a long list of technical domains.
- **Candidate-unfriendly competency grids** (the MSF pool profile is an internal HR document with typos, published as-is).
- Positive model: 4D's application section (documents, deadline with time, how to submit, selection timeline, data protection, no fees) is the most candidate-useful closing seen in the whole group.

### (g) Value-add opportunities for an AI writer (keeping the field-profile voice)

- **Keep the operational third person and the functional grouping.** Candidates in this format scan by function (supply chain, security, team management).
- **Make "Conditions" a required, structured block**: accompanied or unaccompanied; hardship level (as the employer states it); R&R cycle; home leave; housing (individual or shared guesthouse); utilities and connectivity; movement restrictions; travel share; insecurity level. If a field is unknown, say "not stated by employer" rather than inventing it, and **never import a third-party aggregator's metadata as employer fact**.
- **Write a 3-line duty-station summary**: base, roving pattern and access.
- **Limit context to about 120 words** and put the role summary first.
- **Find and remove leftover template text** (checkbox remnants, "undefined", instructions in brackets, duplicated paragraphs) and check title versus body.
- **Translate competency models for candidates.** Keep NRC's two-part model and MSF's competency labels, but turn each into one plain sentence on what will be tested at interview.
- **Use the MSF national-post application block as a template for all formats**: required documents, deadline with time and time zone, how to submit, contact window, data protection, no fees.
- **Flag requirement inflation** (years asked versus grade or salary band) to the employer as a suggestion, not a silent change.

## Cross-cutting notes for the AI writer

- **Safeguarding and Misconduct Disclosure Scheme text appears in every format and nearly every advert** (all four formats; MSF uses its own zero-tolerance wording). The wording is close to identical across Oxfam, Plan, Christian Aid, CARE UK and Mercy Corps. The writer should keep it word for word (it is legal/compliance text) but display it after the role content.
- **Salary**: of the 15 adverts read, only CARE UK (and Oxfam's HQ advert, seen in part) gave a figure. Every other advert gave a grade, a scale reference, "competitive", or nothing. The biggest single gain per line read is a salary band or the grade's local range.
- **Closing date with time zone** is reliable only at SCI (UTC), Oxfam International, NRC (UTC) and MSF. The others give the date alone, "rolling", or put the date at the bottom.
- **Accompanied/unaccompanied, R&R and travel percentage** decide whether field candidates apply. Only formats 4 and 1 regularly state travel, and few adverts state R&R.
- **Duties copied across roles**: SCI's behaviours, Oxfam's "What we are looking for" opener, Plan's organisation paragraph and NRC's generic responsibilities are reused unchanged. That's acceptable as house style, but the writer should add role-specific lines.
- **"Apply even if"** wording: none of the 15 adverts used it. The nearest were equivalence routes (experience instead of a degree in Oxfam 2A and SCI 1B) and Christian Aid's welcome to people from outside the voluntary sector.
