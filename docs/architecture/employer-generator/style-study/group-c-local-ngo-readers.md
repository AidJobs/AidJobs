# Group C: local and national NGOs, domestic charities, and their readers

Desk research for the AidJobs AI job-description writer. Read-only; researched 7 November 2026.

**How to read this file.** Part 1 describes what real adverts look like. Part 2 tests the founder's hypothesis against evidence. Part 3 ranks ideas for the AI writer. The file ends with a 10-line summary.

**Method and limits.**
- Word counts are approximate. I counted them by hand from the text as I read it.
- Several adverts were read on aggregator reposts (Corporate Staffing, JobSpring, Yeshub, World NGO Jobs). Reposts sometimes lose formatting or add text, and this is noted where it matters.
- Two pages refused automated reading (NGOBox and the Ugandan Jobline full page, both with error 403). For those, I used the search-engine extract, and they are marked "partial read".
- Advert text is summarised in my own words. Quotes are short standard phrases.
- Evidence strength is labelled **[academic]**, **[official statistics]**, **[platform or vendor data]** (usually correlational, from a company that sells hiring products, and not peer reviewed), or **[weak]** (a survey or claim with an unclear method, or a news report).

#### Length band
- The adverts are **not uniformly short. Their length is bimodal.**
  - A short cluster of about 150 to 300 words (A1, A3). These usually leave out the duties.
  - A long cluster of about 550 to 850 words (A4, A5, A6, A10, A11). These are padded by an organisation section, template competences and repeated bullets, yet still leave out practical facts.
- The median of the 11 is about 450 words.
- The organisation section often takes 25 to 50% of the words.
- Platforms help: DevNetJobsIndia and bdjobs show title, organisation, location and deadline as structured fields above the text.

#### Voice
- Mostly third person ("The Field Officer will...", "She will...") or "we invite".
- "You" is rare.
- Sentences are long in the organisation section (25 to 40 words) and short in bullets (8 to 15 words).
- The vocabulary is dense with jargon and acronyms: SBCC, PDM, FGD, AAP, IMAM, MIS, DOT.

#### Common weaknesses
1. **Missing practical facts**: pay, hours, travel, housing, start date, and the stages of selection.
2. **Vague or absent duties** in short adverts. Generic duties copied from templates in long ones ("liaise with stakeholders", "any other duty").
3. **Copy-paste and template residue**: repeated bullets, a foreign-language leftover, a title that does not match the body, and two deadlines in one advert.
4. **Requirement inflation**: postgraduate degrees for field posts, grant management for a 30-day deployment, "donor regulations" for mobilisers. At the same time, **the truly essential local language is under-specified**.
5. **Trust gaps**: free-mail addresses, no no-fee notice, aggregator reposts that change content or sell CV services.
6. **Very short windows** (3 days in A10) and rolling deadlines that disadvantage candidates who learn of the post second-hand.

#### Length band
- Body text runs about 230 to 450 words, with the **essential facts in platform header fields** and the full job description as an attachment.
- In effect this is already a layered advert: a fact header, a short story, then the attachment.

#### Voice
- "We" and "you", warm and plain, with sentences of 12 to 20 words. There is more selling of the cause and of benefits than in Global South adverts.

#### Content rules
- Pay is near-universal. CharityJob requires it for UK roles, and 88% of surveyed UK charities included it ([fundraising.co.uk on CharityJob research](https://fundraising.co.uk/2022/01/21/fairness-in-recruitment-gaining-importance-but-still-a-way-to-go/) **[platform or vendor data]**).
- Hours, contract, closing date and time, and workplace mode (on site, hybrid, remote) are standard.
- Benefits are listed in the US and Australia.
- Selection steps and interview dates are often missing.
- Safeguarding checks are often in the attachment rather than the advert.

#### Weaknesses
- Facts that matter are hidden in attachments (PDFs are costly on mobile).
- Catch-all duty lines.
- Mission-heavy openings.
- Missing interview dates and safeguarding-check notes.
- Typos in pay figures.

**Key contrast.** Domestic platforms *force* the top facts into structured fields, so the body can stay short. Most Global South NGO adverts have no such scaffold. **The AI writer can supply the scaffold.**

## Part 2. Readers and evidence

### 2.0 Verdict on the founder's hypothesis

The hypothesis is that local NGOs keep adverts short because they lack skill, or because their hires would not read long ones. **It is partly supported, but it misframes the problem.**

1. **Local adverts are not mainly short; they are badly allocated.** The long ones in Part 1 spend 25 to 50% of their words on the organisation and on repeated template text, and the short ones omit duties. Both kinds omit pay, hours, travel and selection.
2. **Skill and capacity are real constraints**, as the copy-paste errors, conflicting deadlines and template residue show.
   - Dasra found that 33% of Indian nonprofits with up to 100 staff had no dedicated talent-management team ([India Development Review](https://idronline.org/heres-how-you-can-hire-and-retain-talent-better/) **[weak: 97 survey responses]**).
   - A Ugandan scoping report says recruitment is often done by the general manager or owner through personal networks ([Challenge Fund for Youth Employment, Uganda scoping](https://fundforyouthemployment.nl/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/Challenge-Fund-for-Youth-Employment-Uganda-Scoping-Report.pdf) **[weak: qualitative]**).
3. **Some omissions are deliberate, not unskilled.** In Addis Ababa, only about 4% of job adverts include pay. Firms said that posting a wage would attract under-qualified applicants (41% of vacancies) or too many applicants (32%), or would remove room to negotiate (41%). ([Balgova, Tekleselassie, Hensel and Witte 2025, IZA DP 18220](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.5684443) **[academic]**.) The writer therefore has to *persuade and offer safe formats* (a range, a scale, a "from" figure, allowances), not just add a field.
4. **Habit from paid print is plausible.** Indian newspaper job adverts are priced per line or per square centimetre ([Riyo Advertising price guide](https://riyoadvertising.com/current-info/job-ad-cost-in-indian-newspapers-2026-price-guide/); [Baba Media HT rate card](https://babamediagroup.com/recruitment-advertisement-in-newspaper-ht/) **[weak: agency pages]**), and Kenyan print classifieds were priced per line ([Daily Nation](https://nation.africa/kenya/business/-nation-classified-goes-online-607258) **[weak: old news]**). This is inference, not tested evidence.
5. **"Readers will not read long adverts" is half true.** Readers scan, read on phones, often read a forwarded copy, and some read slowly or in a second language (sections 2.1 and 2.2). What they need is **the decisive facts first, in plain words**, not fewer facts.

### 2.1 How job seekers read adverts (general evidence, mostly US and UK)

**Length against applications.** The sources conflict, all are vendor data and all are correlational:
- LinkedIn: posts of 150 words or fewer drew applications 17.8% more often than posts of 450 to 600 words ([LinkedIn ebook](https://business.linkedin.com/content/dam/me/business/en-us/talent-solutions/resources/pdfs/job-descriptions-ebook.pdf) **[platform or vendor data]**).
- Appcast: highest apply rate at 201 to 400 words (8 to 8.5%); about 4.5% under 200 words; below 5% over 700 words ([Appcast](https://www.appcast.io/how-to-write-a-job-ad-that-converts/) **[platform or vendor data]**). Appcast's 2018 report had found 300 to 800 words best ([2018 benchmark](https://cdn2.hubspot.net/hubfs/1859609/FINAL%20CONTENT%20PDFS/Reports/2018%20Recruitment%20Media%20Benchmark%20Report%20%5BAppcast%5D.pdf)).
- Indeed: 700 to 2,000 characters (roughly 110 to 330 words) get up to 30% more applications ([Indeed](https://www.indeed.com/lead/reasons-great-applicants-abandon-the-process) **[platform or vendor data]**).
- Textio: 300 to 660 words fill fastest, with 13 to 18 words per sentence ([Textio](https://textio.com/blog/how-to-write-a-job-description-best-practices-from-half-a-billion-job-postings) **[platform or vendor data]**; it measures time to fill, not apply rate).
- CharityJob: about 500 words, with candidates 15% more likely to apply ([CIOF, citing CharityJob](https://ciof.org.uk/events-and-training/resources/secrets-to-job-ad-success-eight-tips-to-get-your-r) **[platform or vendor data]**).

*Reading of this:* very long adverts underperform. Very short ones may also underperform (Appcast). A sensible target is **about 150 to 450 words with the key facts first**. Raising the apply rate is not the same as getting better applicants. No Global South length study was found.

**Scanning.**
- Web users read roughly 20% of the words on an average page ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-little-do-users-read/) **[academic-style usability research]**).
- They concentrate on the first lines and the first words of each line ([NN/g F-pattern](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/f-shaped-pattern-reading-web-content/)).
- **Lower-literacy readers do not scan. They read word by word and skip large blocks when text gets dense** ([NN/g, lower-literacy users](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/writing-for-lower-literacy-users/)). The same rewrite helped both lower- and higher-literacy users in their test.

**What candidates look for first.**
- In a LinkedIn survey, the most helpful parts were compensation (61%), then qualifications and job details (49% each), well above culture or mission ([LinkedIn ebook](https://business.linkedin.com/content/dam/me/business/en-us/talent-solutions/resources/pdfs/job-descriptions-ebook.pdf) **[platform or vendor data]**).
- CharityJob candidates' main pain points were long application forms (59%), not hearing back (54%), cover letters (40%), too many requirements (34%) and salary not matching the responsibilities (29%) ([CharityJob](https://www.charityjob.co.uk/careeradvice/improve-recruitment/) **[platform or vendor data]**).

**Mobile.**
- 75% of applications in Appcast's 2024 US data came from mobile, higher for jobs done on one's feet ([Appcast 2025 benchmark](https://info.appcast.io/hubfs/FINAL%20CONTENT%20PDFS/Whitepapers/%5BWhitepaper%5D%20Appcast%20Recruitment%20Benchmark%20Report%202025.pdf) **[platform or vendor data]**).
- Applications that take over 15 minutes convert at 3.6%, against 12.5% for 1 to 5 minutes ([Appcast](https://www.appcast.io/how-to-write-a-job-ad-that-converts/)).
- Application forms cut applicant numbers by over 50% compared with CVs ([CIOF and CharityJob](https://ciof.org.uk/events-and-training/resources/secrets-to-job-ad-success-eight-tips-to-get-your-r)).

**Tone.** In a LinkedIn test, a very casual advert was disliked and 2 to 4 times less likely to be applied to than a plain one ([LinkedIn ebook](https://business.linkedin.com/content/dam/me/business/en-us/talent-solutions/resources/pdfs/job-descriptions-ebook.pdf) **[platform or vendor data]**).

### 2.2 Job seekers in South Asia and Africa

**Mobile, data and devices** ([GSMA State of Mobile Internet Connectivity 2025](https://www.gsma.com/somic/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/The-State-of-Mobile-Internet-Connectivity-2025-Overview-Report.pdf); [GSMA Mobile Gender Gap 2025](https://www.gsma.com/newsroom/press-release/progress-closing-the-mobile-internet-gender-gap-stalls-in-lmics-gsma-mobile-gender-gap-report-2025/) **[official or industry statistics]**):
- Only about 25% of people in Sub-Saharan Africa use mobile internet.
- In 63% of low- and middle-income countries, 1 GB of data is unaffordable for the poorest 40%.
- An entry-level smartphone costs 87% of monthly income for the poorest fifth in Sub-Saharan Africa.
- Women are 14% less likely than men to use mobile internet, and the gap is 32% in South Asia and 29% in Sub-Saharan Africa.
- *Implication:* heavy pages, PDFs and video CVs exclude people. Many field candidates, especially women, read on a shared or borrowed phone, or have the advert relayed to them by someone else.

**Word of mouth and networks.**
- 60% of working-age Ugandans look for work through friends and relatives, and 16% through newspaper adverts ([Daily Monitor on the Uganda Bureau of Statistics 2021 labour force survey](https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/60-ugandans-search-jobs-through-connections-report-4024720) **[official statistics, via news]**).
- Among actively searching Ugandan youth, 42.4% asked friends and relatives, and 0.7% used employment centres ([ILO school-to-work survey, Uganda](https://www.ilo.org/sites/default/files/wcmsp5/groups/public/@dgreports/@dcomm/documents/publication/wcms_326255.pdf) **[official statistics, 2013]**).
- Kenyan youth most often enquire in person at workplaces ([University of Nairobi thesis](https://erepository.uonbi.ac.ke/handle/11295/166650) **[academic, thesis]**).
- In Addis Ababa, a key method is visiting **physical job boards in the city centre**, which is costly for people who live far away, and 41% of applicants had applied without a CV ([J-PAL summary of Abebe et al., Review of Economic Studies](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/evaluation/anonymity-or-distance-removing-obstacles-youth-employment-urban-ethiopia) **[academic]**).

**WhatsApp and Telegram.**
- Job seekers in Uganda find openings on a few job sites, in **informal WhatsApp groups** and in newspapers; LinkedIn is hardly used, and over 80% of one site's jobs were in Kampala ([CFYE Uganda scoping](https://fundforyouthemployment.nl/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/Challenge-Fund-for-Youth-Employment-Uganda-Scoping-Report.pdf) **[weak: qualitative]**).
- One Bengaluru volunteer runs WhatsApp groups for HR staff and a Telegram group of over 13,000 job seekers, and warns members not to pay fees ([The Better India](https://thebetterindia.com/246827/bengaluru-job-search-unemployment-youth-achuthananda-hr-social-media-linkedin-whatsapp-san196/) **[weak: news anecdote]**).
- Job posts circulate in Kano women's WhatsApp groups ([Global Citizen](https://www.globalcitizen.org/en/content/nigeria-women-whatsapp-earn/) **[weak]**).
- About 1 in 6 working-age women in India, Kenya, Nigeria and Pakistan use WhatsApp for their livelihoods ([Caribou](https://caribou.global/projects/soc-com/); [Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/05/15/2026/nearly-60-of-self-employed-women-in-nigeria-use-whatsapp-for-work) **[weak or moderate: consultancy survey]**).
- Part 1 shows what happens when adverts travel through reposts: formatting is lost (A6), facts change (A7, A11) and paid services get attached (A7).
- I found **no quantitative study of how often NGO adverts specifically are shared on WhatsApp**. This is a gap.

**Local language.**
- 98% of India's 886 million internet users accessed content in Indic languages in 2024, and 57% of urban users prefer Indic content ([IAMAI and Kantar, Internet in India 2024](https://www.iamai.in/sites/default/files/research/Kantar%5F%20IAMAI%20report%5F2024%5F.pdf) **[industry survey, large sample]**).
- Bangla newspapers republish NGO field circulars in Bangla with a fact table (Part 1, A5).
- In Sudan, national NGO staff are shut out by English-only documents and jargon ([CSF working paper on localisation in Sudan](https://dzo.kei.mybluehost.me/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/making-sense-of-localisation-in-sudan-csf.pdf) **[weak or moderate: qualitative]**).
- Violet's "CV in English only" rule for a Hama community post (A11) shows the same barrier at the application stage.

**Literacy.**
- Adult literacy in 2024 was 69% in Sub-Saharan Africa and 77% in Central and Southern Asia. India has 191 million illiterate adults, Nigeria 43 million and Bangladesh 25 million ([UNESCO International Literacy Day 2025 factsheet](https://www.unesco.org/sites/default/files/medias/fichiers/2025/09/ild-2025-factsheet.pdf) **[official statistics, self-reported basic literacy]**).
- No source measured literacy among applicants for field roles. Most posts in Part 1 require school leaving or more, so applicants are literate, but many read English as a second or third language. **Plain English and short sentences matter more than brevity.**

**What is missing, in candidates' terms.**
- In the Addis Ababa study, job adverts usually had the title and education requirement, but tasks, skill requirements and pay were rarely included ([Balgova et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.5684443) **[academic]**).
- A Nigerian development professional told Devex he would not have applied to a job that required moving cities without the salary in the advert ([Devex](https://www.devex.com/news/development-organizations-face-calls-for-more-salary-transparency-103610) **[weak: anecdote]**).
- I found no systematic survey of Global South candidates on what NGO adverts lack. This is a gap; AidJobs could run one.

### 2.3 Salary transparency

- **Slovakia** made pay in adverts compulsory in 2018. Afterwards, the firms that newly showed pay got more clicks and applications, and new hires' wages rose by about 3%. Applicants were not better matched, and the gender gap did not close ([Škoda, job-market paper](https://samuelskoda.github.io/skoda%5Fjmp.pdf) **[academic, quasi-experimental]**).
- **Ethiopia field experiment.** Adding pay did not change the *average* number of applicants, but it re-sorted them: higher-paid posts drew more and lower-paid posts fewer. Average quality was unchanged, and pay attracted people with **higher job-search costs who lived further away**, which is the group AidJobs most wants to reach ([Balgova et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.5684443) **[academic, randomised]**). This is the most relevant study for local NGOs. Pay helps candidates decide; it does not guarantee more applications.
- **Vendor and platform data:**
  - Indeed: postings with employer-provided pay get 3.8 times more applications ([Indeed](https://www.indeed.com/news/releases/pay-transparency-across-high-earning-sectors)).
  - CharityJob: about twice as many successful applications ([CharityJob](https://www.charityjob.co.uk/recruiterinsights/build-a-fairer-charity-sector/)).
  - CharityComms survey: 92% said "competitive" or no salary would put them off ([We Show The Salary](https://www.weshowthesalary.org.uk/post/why-should-job-boards-show-the-salary-charitycomms)).
  - Heart Talent: 75% of Australian not-for-profit adverts lack pay ([Heart Talent](https://www.hearttalent.com.au/blog/the-state-of-nfp-employer-branding-job-advertising)).
  - All four are **[platform or vendor data]**, likely overstated through selection effects.
- **Aid sector norms:** Bond made salaries mandatory on its jobs board ([Bond](https://www.bond.org.uk/press-releases/2020/10/sector-needs-greater-salary-transparency-to-close-pay-gap-says-bond-who-signs-up-to-pledge-to-disclose-salaries-on-its-jobs-board/)).
- **Context for local NGOs:** local NGOs lose trained staff to better-paying INGOs and UN agencies ([CSF Sudan](https://dzo.kei.mybluehost.me/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/making-sense-of-localisation-in-sudan-csf.pdf); [CDA and Peace Direct consultation](https://cnxus.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/P4D-Consultation-Report_Final.pdf) **[weak or moderate: qualitative]**). Some may hide pay because it compares badly. Honest pay plus non-pay facts (allowances, contract security, training, posting near home) is the fair answer.

### 2.4 Inclusive wording

- **Gaucher, Friesen and Kay (2011):** in experiments, masculine-coded wording made jobs less appealing to women by lowering their sense of belonging ([doi:10.1037/a0022530](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0022530) **[academic, lab]**).
- **Castilla and Rho (2023)**, with about 296,000 real postings and a field experiment, found **negligible practical effects** of gendered wording on who applies ([Management Science](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2023.4674); [MIT Sloan summary](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/ideas-made-to-matter/altering-gendered-language-job-postings-doesnt-help-attract-more-women) **[academic, field]**).
- *Implication:* run a cheap check for coded words, but do not sell it as a major feature. Explicit, concrete inclusion does matter, for example "women strongly encouraged" where the role needs female staff (A1, A4), or accessibility facts.
- None of these studies were done in South Asian or African languages.

### 2.5 Requirement inflation

- 67% of US production-supervisor postings asked for a degree, while only 16% of the people doing the job had one; more than 6 million jobs were at risk of degree inflation ([HBS, Dismissed by Degrees](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/dismissed-by-degrees_707b3f0e-a772-40b7-8f77-aed4a16016cc.pdf) **[academic report with a survey]**).
- Women apply to 20% fewer jobs and are 16% less likely to apply after viewing one ([LinkedIn Gender Insights](https://business.linkedin.com/content/dam/me/business/en-us/talent-solutions-lodestone/body/pdf/Gender-Insights-Report.pdf) **[platform or vendor data]**). The widely quoted "100% against 60% of criteria" line in that report comes from a single internal HP report and is **[weak]**.
- 34% of CharityJob candidates complain of too many requirements ([CharityJob](https://www.charityjob.co.uk/careeradvice/improve-recruitment/)).
- Caution from Ethiopia: telling candidates the required skill level did not improve how well they matched themselves to jobs ([Balgova et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.5684443)). So cutting requirements widens the pool but does not by itself filter it.
- Part 1 has clear inflation cases (A3, A6, A10) alongside **under-specified essentials** such as the local language (A10, A11).

### 2.6 Fake job scams and trust

- **India (Lucknow):** a fake NGO took fee deposits of roughly ₹90,000 to ₹1.1 lakh from about 150 people, with fake appointment letters for a "TB awareness project" ([Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/lucknow/fake-ngo-gives-fake-job-letter-to-150-people-dupe-them-of-rs-1-crore/articleshow/130153425.cms) **[news]**).
- **Kenya:** fake "international NGO" messages demanding KSh 1,800 for "training" ([News Light Kenya](https://newslightkenya.co.ke/public-warned-over-fake-ngo-recruitment-scam/)), and a UNICEF warning ([Nairobi Leo](https://nairobileo.co.ke/news/article/30110/unicef-kenya-issues-warning-over-fake-job-offers-recruitment-scams)) **[news]**.
- **Nigeria:** FAO impersonation with a look-alike domain and a fake $99 "certificate" ([FactCheck Africa](https://factcheckafrica.net/beware-a-job-too-good-to-be-true-how-scammers-exploited-the-fao-brand-to-prey-on-nigerian-job-seekers/)), and a local NGO warning that its name is used with a free-mail address ([HEI Nigeria](https://hei.org.ng/important-notice-beware-of-fraudulent-recruitment-activities/)).
- **Standard trust signals** in UN and INGO notices are: no fee at any stage (application, interview, training, travel), only official domains, and a list of where real vacancies are posted ([UN](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/fraud-alert); [UNHCR](https://www.unhcr.org/get-involved/work-us/careers-unhcr/how-avoid-scams-and-fraudulent-job-offers); [WFP](https://www.wfp.org/careers/fraudulent-job-offers); [UNICEF](https://www.unicef.org/careers/beware-fraudulent-job-offers); [World Vision Malawi](https://www.wvi.org/newsroom/malawi/recruitment-fraud-notice)).
- Scams copy real formats, including the real free BSAFE training. So **verifiability** (an official link, an official domain, a reference code, a verified-organisation badge) matters more than a warning sentence alone.
- Only 1 of the 11 local adverts carried a no-fee line, and several use free-mail addresses. Scammers use free-mail too.

### 2.7 What small NGO hiring managers struggle with

- **US:** 74.6% of nonprofits reported vacancies, most often in programme and service-delivery roles (74%). Salary competition (72.2%) and budget limits (66.3%) were the top barriers ([National Council of Nonprofits 2023](https://www.councilofnonprofits.org/files/media/documents/2023/2023-nonprofit-workforce-survey-results.pdf) **[survey, n=1,600+]**).
- **India:** remote locations, emotionally hard work, thin budgets and a mix of frontline and specialist staff. Only 18% could invest adequately in organisational development ([India Development Review, citing Dasra and Bridgespan](https://idronline.org/heres-how-you-can-hire-and-retain-talent-better/); [IDR, QUEST case study](https://idronline.org/article/scale/case-study-to-scale-or-not-to-scale/) **[weak or moderate]**).
- **Ethiopia:** employers' own stated fear is attracting *too many or under-qualified* applicants ([Balgova et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.5684443)).
- **Mixed picture:** posts in remote districts may get too few suitable applicants, while generic city posts get floods. That argues for **precise must-haves and practical facts**, which help candidates rule themselves in or out, rather than padding.
- I found no direct evidence on how long small NGO managers spend writing adverts. This is a gap.

## Part 3. Value-add design for short adverts

**Ranking principle:** value per word added to the advert the candidate sees. Some ideas add zero words to the advert, either because they are prompts to the employer or because they are separate outputs.

| Rank | Idea | Words added | Why (evidence) | Also helps INGOs? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Flag missing facts; never invent them.** Before drafting, check for pay, hours or shifts, posting and travel, contract length, start date, selection steps and a no-fee line, and ask the employer. Where a fact is unknown, show a visible placeholder or leave it out rather than fill it. | 0 | The facts candidates decide on are the ones most often missing (Part 1: pay 2 of 11, selection steps 0 of 11). AI invention would mislead people and break trust. | Yes |
| 2 | **Top card of 6 to 8 facts** at the top, plain text: role and number of posts; posting and travel area; pay (figure, range, scale or allowance); contract and duration; hours or shifts; deadline and how to apply; start date; 1 or 2 must-haves. | About 40 to 70 | People read the first lines and the left edge, and lower-literacy readers stop early (NN/g). Candidates want pay, requirements and details first (LinkedIn). Bangla reposts and UK charity platforms already use this pattern. | Yes |
| 3 | **A pay line with safe formats**: "₹X to Y a month plus travel and daily allowance", "As per [named scale]", "Stipend ₹X a day" or "Unpaid volunteer; transport refunded". Offer this as a choice; if the employer refuses, flag it. | About 8 to 15 | The strongest single fact. Causal studies show more applications or better sorting (Škoda; Balgova et al.). Vendor data claim 2 to 4 times as many. Ethiopia shows it reaches candidates with high search costs. | Yes |
| 4 | **A trust line plus verification**: "[Org] never charges a fee at any stage. Apply only via [official link or domain]." Show the reference code and a verified-organisation badge, and warn the employer about free-mail apply addresses. | About 15 to 20 | Fee scams are documented in India, Kenya and Nigeria, and only 1 of 11 local adverts had the line. | Yes |
| 5 | **Realistic must-haves (at most 3 to 5) against nice-to-haves**, with prompts on inflation ("Is a postgraduate degree truly needed for this field post?") and promotion of real essentials (local language, licence class, living in the area). | Usually **negative** | Degree inflation (HBS). Fewer applications from women (LinkedIn, moderate). Candidate complaints (CharityJob). Part 1 cases A3, A6, A10. | Yes |
| 6 | **Specific duties instead of vague ones**: 4 to 7 duties written as verb, object, frequency and place (for example, "Run 2 self-help group meetings a week in 6 villages; record attendance on a tablet"). Drop repeated and catch-all lines. | Neutral or negative | Fixes both the empty short advert and the padded long one. Concrete duties are what the LinkedIn survey calls "job details". | Yes |
| 7 | **Field practicalities block, filled only where it applies**: travel days and mode (motorbike, public transport), licence, spoken against written language, living at the posting or housing, field hours and nights away, security conditions, phone or device provided. | About 20 to 45 | These decide whether a field candidate can take the job; they appear in only a few adverts (A3, A6, A9). Real employers already write such facts (New Incentives Nigeria mentions travel by public transport to remote clinics: https://new-incentives.breezy.hr/p/d0d0e6ea8f4f-field-officers-entry-level-all-locations). | Yes, for national field posts |
| 8 | **Selection-process line**: "Written test and interview in [town] around [date]. Shortlisted applicants are contacted by [date]; if you have not heard by then, assume you were not selected." | About 20 to 30 | Turns "only shortlisted contacted" into something candidates can plan around. Not hearing back is a top pain point (54%, CharityJob). It also helps candidates spot scams, since a real process has a known shape. | Yes |
| 9 | **WhatsApp-ready short version** (a separate output of 60 to 90 words in plain text): the top-card facts, the no-fee line, the reference code, the official link and the deadline. No emojis, images or attachments. | 0 (separate) | Matches how field candidates receive adverts (WhatsApp and Telegram, word of mouth). Limits the damage from reposts that alter content (A6, A7, A11). Light on data. | Yes, for national hiring |
| 10 | **Plain-language pass**: sentences of 13 to 18 words, acronyms spelt out, no jargon, readable at roughly school-grade 6 to 8, and "you" rather than "the incumbent". | Neutral | Lower-literacy and second-language readers read word by word (NN/g). Textio's sentence-length data agree. | Yes |
| 11 | **Organisation section cut to 1 or 2 sentences plus a link** (what the organisation does here, and since when). | **Negative** (often minus 50 to 300) | It takes 25 to 50% of words in Part 1 and ranks low among what candidates want (company mission 27% in the LinkedIn survey). | Yes |
| 12 | **Local-language version** (a separate output, such as Hindi, Bangla, Swahili, Hausa or Arabic). It should cover at least the top card and duties; the employer approves it, and it is labelled as a translation with the English as the reference. | 0 (separate) | Indic-language preference (IAMAI). Bangla reposts already exist. English-only rules exclude local candidates. The cost of checking it ranks it below the items above. | Sometimes (national posts) |
| 13 | **One safeguarding and checks line** where the role involves children or vulnerable adults: "Includes reference and police checks; [Org] follows a child-protection policy." | About 15 to 20 | Expected, and also a legitimacy signal. Long safeguarding paragraphs (A5) can shrink to this plus a link. | Yes |
| 14 | **A suggested low-bandwidth way to apply**: a short CV by email or WhatsApp to an *official* number, hand delivery to a named office, no video CV and no long forms. | About 10 | Forms and long applications cut applications sharply (Appcast; CharityJob). Data costs (GSMA). The official-number condition avoids opening a scam route. | Partly |
| 15 | **Outcome statements** ("In your first 3 months you will..."). Use only when the employer supplies them. | About 25 to 40 | Useful for officer and manager roles. Low value for driver, nurse or enumerator posts. High risk of AI invention, so it ranks low. | Yes, for professional roles |

### What would not help, or would backfire

- **Making adverts longer to look "proper"**: mission essays, competency frameworks, repeated equal-opportunity or safeguarding paragraphs. The evidence penalises length without content.
- **Invented specifics**: pay, benefits, outcomes, dates or selection steps the employer did not give. They mislead, they damage trust in AidJobs, and they look like scam adverts.
- **Selling gender-neutral wording as a headline feature.** The field evidence shows negligible effects (Castilla and Rho). Keep it as a silent check.
- **A very casual or hyped tone, emojis, or "urgent!!" in the WhatsApp version.** These look like scam messages, and casual tone tested badly (LinkedIn). Appcast found that titles with 2 or more symbols got about 30% fewer applications (vendor).
- **Machine translation without the employer's review**, especially of pay, contract terms or legal checks.
- **Lowering real requirements** (licence class, professional registration) in the name of inclusion.
- **Removing "only shortlisted contacted"** without giving a date instead, which sets expectations the NGO cannot meet.
- **Pushing for one exact pay figure** where it depends on a donor budget. Offer ranges, scales or "from" figures instead, or the NGO will leave the field blank or abandon the tool.
- **Asking for a phone or WhatsApp number to apply to a personal number.** That copies scam patterns. Insist on an official number or domain.
- **Over-structuring a 6-line advert** with many headings. The top card plus 2 or 3 short blocks is enough.

### Suggested target lengths (to test, not settled)

| Output | Target |
|---|---|
| Top card | 40 to 70 words |
| Full advert, field roles | About 200 to 400 words |
| Full advert, officer and manager roles | About 350 to 600 words |
| WhatsApp version | 60 to 90 words, under about 600 characters |

These rest on US and UK vendor data and on reading-behaviour research. AidJobs should A/B-test them on its own traffic.

## Summary of recommendations (10 lines)

1. The hypothesis is only half right. Local NGO adverts are not mainly short; they put words in the wrong places (organisation and template text) and leave out decisive facts.
2. The writer's first job is to **ask for missing facts and never invent them**: pay, hours, posting and travel, contract, start date, selection steps.
3. Lead every advert with a **6-to-8-fact top card**. Bangla newspaper reposts and UK charity platforms already show the pattern works.
4. Push hard for a **pay line**, offering safe formats (range, scale, "from", allowances, stipend). The best causal evidence says it helps candidates sort themselves, especially distant ones.
5. Add a **no-fee and verification line**, with the official link and reference code. Fee scams using NGO names are documented in India, Kenya and Nigeria.
6. Trim **must-haves to 3 to 5 that are really essential**; challenge degree and experience inflation; promote the real essentials, such as the local language.
7. Replace vague duties with **4 to 7 concrete duties**, and add a **field practicalities block** only where it applies.
8. Turn "only shortlisted contacted" into a **dated selection line**.
9. Produce a **WhatsApp version** (60 to 90 words, plain text, official link) and, where the employer approves it, a **local-language version** as separate outputs.
10. Most of this helps INGOs too. Avoid padding, hype, emojis, unreviewed translation, invented outcomes, and treating gender-word tweaks as a major feature.
