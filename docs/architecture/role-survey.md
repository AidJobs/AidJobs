## 1. Purpose

AidJobs' job-description generator (held until the crawler foundation is done; the user, 8 November 2026) needs role templates: for each kind of job, what is actually written under each heading, how it changes by organisation type, level and context, and what would make AidJobs' version better than the best real adverts rather than merely adequate. The demo's 13 role families (`demo/employer`, `engine.ts`, `ROLE_FAMILIES`: WASH Programme Manager, Health Programme Officer, Child Protection Specialist, Education Officer, MEAL Coordinator, Finance Officer, Logistics Officer, Communications Officer, ESG and Safeguards Specialist, Climate Resilience Adviser, Livelihoods Officer, HR Officer, Programme Manager) were written by the agent with no evidence behind them: 12 on 3 November 2026 (demo round 2, the Create a job page, commit `ed0d453`), and the 13th, ESG and Safeguards Specialist, on 9 November 2026 (`0344882`). They are placeholders, to be replaced by this survey's results; they are not its starting point, and the demo is not changed now (the user, 10 November 2026).

The survey answers four questions, in order:
1. **Which roles** do the organisations AidJobs serves actually advertise, how often, at which levels and in which contexts (Phase 1)?
2. **How are their adverts structured** by organisation type: sections, order, length, facts given and missing, standard text (Phase 2)?
3. **What is written** under each heading, unit by unit, how specific it is, what each role's best adverts say that its weakest leave out, and what the profession's own standards expect (Phase 3)?
4. **What should AidJobs' templates contain** so that, scored blind against real adverts, they beat the best quarter for each role (Phases 4 and 5)?

The answers are delivered in two forms: readable findings for people, and a machine-readable role library (section 9) that the AI chat reads to recognise a role, ask for its signature facts, and write to the best quarter's standard.

## 3. Rules and known limits

**Rules (the user's, 10 November 2026, with the employer survey's rules where they apply):**
- **Read-only.** Every request goes through the backend's shared fetch policy (`contracts/outbound.py`) with the survey's honest identity (`SURVEY_USER_AGENT`, `AidJobsSurvey/0.1 (+mailto:aidjobz@gmail.com)`), GET or HEAD only. `robots.txt` is read first and judged with the RFC 9309 matcher (`contracts/robots.py`); a page it forbids is not requested; a `robots.txt` that cannot be reached forbids the host, as RFC 9309 says. A 403, a 429 or a bot challenge is recorded and stops that organisation for the run, never solved or bypassed. The stop rule is step F's as changed by the user on 6 November 2026: three refusals in a row from one host or one protection service, five in a row from any sites, or five network failures in a row.
- **One exception, the model test round** (section 10): POST calls to OpenRouter's chat API, made by a separate script outside the fetch policy, approved per round with its budget. They send AidJobs' own text only (the briefs' facts and units, the library, the instructions), never a page fetched for the survey or any advert's text.
- **Each site's terms respected.** A site whose terms forbid automated access is not read by any script (as UNjobs is not). Job boards and mirrors are used only after their terms are checked and recorded.
- **Each batch of requests is approved by the user before it runs**, with its list of hosts, the number of requests, and its expected time.
- **No advert text copied.** Committed records hold counts, categories, and the agent's own paraphrases of at most 12 words; standard sector phrases may be quoted when under 15 words, in quotation marks and marked *(std)*. A local check, run before each commit, refuses any committed paraphrase, template line, library line or reference job description, and any brief's unit, that matches an 8-word run of a saved page (quotes marked *(std)* exempt). Reference job descriptions use a placeholder for an organisation's standard text (its safeguarding, UN Considerations or equal-opportunity blocks), never a real organisation's wording.
- **No page content committed beyond what the employer survey's privacy rules allow** (`source-survey.md`, "Privacy rules": no page content from employers' sites is committed). Job titles are therefore committed only as counts per organisation by role, and as title variants pooled across organisations (section 6); individual titles stay local. Committing titles themselves would need the user to amend that rule (section 15).
- **No recruiter names, emails or phone numbers** (growth plan 4.6). Contact details are removed from saved text before coding, by a step of the survey's own in `tools/survey/` with tests (or by the crawler's cleaner once W-37's capture, which adds one, is merged); a CI test fails if any committed survey file, the library and its reference job descriptions included, holds an email address or a phone-like number; names are never recorded (coders are instructed, and the review checks a sample).
- **Raw pages stay local** in `tools/survey/raw/` (already git-ignored). Only cleaned records are committed. Files a script builds from committed inputs are rebuilt by CI, which fails if they differ, as for steps D to F; files coded by agents (scores, units, template lines) cannot be rebuilt, so CI checks their form instead: the columns, the allowed values, a label on every finding, no email or phone number, quotes under 15 words and marked *(std)*, paraphrases of 12 words or fewer (section 12).
- **Every finding labelled** Fact, Estimate or Unverified, with its source.
- **No production crawl, no write to the jobs database, no reindex.** Reading AidJobs' own stored jobs uses the read-only login, with read-only enforced and proven (operating conventions), and is its own approval.

**Known limits:**
- **ReliefWeb's API** waits on an `appname`, which ReliefWeb grants only to an official organisation address (W-31, open decision 1). Its website pages are used only within its terms, checked first (Phase 1, batch P1-3); its job pages have answered automated reads with 403, 404 and an AWS WAF challenge before (style study; W-04), so they may yield nothing.
- **UN pages and mirrors (the user's exception, 10 November 2026).** `careers.un.org` and some agency sites refused the style study's reads (403). The user allowed mirrors that republish the official text, used and noted. Because a refusal is otherwise never worked around (growth plan 4.2), the exception is narrow: a mirror is used only for a UN page that refused the survey's honest identity once, never where the official site's `robots.txt` forbids the page, and only after the mirror's own terms and `robots.txt` are read and allow it; each use is noted, and the mirror's text compared with the official one where it can be. Mirrors are job boards, so the decision of 4 November 2026 applies to them: `untalent.org` is one of the eight boards it excluded and is not used; others, such as `unzhaopin.com` (used by the style study), only once their terms are read (batch P1-4, section 6).
- **Job boards.** On 4 November 2026 the eight boards checked (Devex, Impactpool, DevNetJobs, DevelopmentAid, UN Talent, Idealist, CharityJob, Bond) did not allow their employer data to be collected, and the user decided to use none (W-31). Phase 1 keeps that decision; other boards are proposed only after their terms are read (batch P1-4), and the user decides.
- **Workday** (20 first-batch employers, 1,225 UNjobs jobs, the largest system) builds its listing with a POST job search, which contract §9 allows only for the crawler's Workday reader (amendment of 6 November 2026). The survey cannot count Workday employers' titles until AidJobs crawls them (growth plan step 6), so Phase 1 under-counts organisations on Workday, Oracle and other script-built listings (61 of step F's first batch). - **Bot protection.** About one employer site in nine refused step F outright (17 bot challenges, 9 refused `robots.txt`). Those employers are missing from the sample, which leans toward sites that accept honest robots.
- **National NGOs in the Global South** are thin in the survey's own list, which comes from UNjobs and Northern registers (`organisations.csv`: 74 in Kenya, the largest Southern country). Phase 2 needs about 50 of their adverts; where they come from is a decision (section 15).
- **One moment in time.** A listing shows current openings only; Phase 1 takes two snapshots about a month apart to reduce that bias. Large employers would dominate counts, so each organisation counts at most 20 titles per snapshot.
- **No practitioners.** The user has no hiring managers to review the templates. Validation is by blind scoring, automatic checks, and later real use (Phase 5); until real use, the templates are labelled "based on evidence, not yet tested with hiring managers".

## 5. Phase 0: the scoring guide

Fixed before any advert is read. Every advert read in Phases 2 and 3 is scored with it, and so is every template output in Phase 5. It is built from the style study's candidate research (`employer-generator/style-study/group-c-local-ngo-readers.md`, part 2, and the weaknesses and value-add sections of groups A and B).

**The target.** For each role family, pooled across organisation types (about 30 adverts in wave 1), the 75th percentile of the total score among real adverts, always stated with its number of adverts. A template passes when its output, for every brief it is tested on, scores at or above that percentile and no dimension scores below the family's median, when scored blind among real adverts (Phase 5). The 75th percentile per organisation type is also reported, as information, where a type has at least 8 adverts. Standard text (commitments, legal and organisation boilerplate, by Phase 2's section types) is set aside, for real adverts and template outputs alike, only where it would distort a comparison: for the readability measures, for the length of role content, and in the blind layout of Phase 5, so neither side gains from it. Decision facts and fairness (its trust and inclusion signals sit in those sections) are scored from the full advert, and the boilerplate share comes from Phase 2's standard-share measure.

**Nine dimensions, each scored 0 to 3** (the weights are the agent's proposal, for the user's decision; they add to 100):

| Dimension | Weight | 0 | 3 | How it is scored |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Decision facts:** the facts candidates decide on (pay as a figure, range, named scale or allowance; contract type and length; duty station and travel or roving share; hours or level of effort where they vary; closing date with time and time zone; start date; selection steps or timeline; how to apply; languages) | 20 | fewer than a third of the applicable facts, no pay | all applicable facts, pay included | automatic from the Phase 2 fact list; pay counts double |
| **Specificity:** duties carry numbers, places, named standards, tools or systems, frequency, counterparts, scale | 15 | generic duties only | most duties carry at least two kinds of specifics | from the specifics in Phase 3's units only, never from the signature facts, which Phase 3 derives from the scores (Phase 2 estimates it from the same specifics, and Phase 3 replaces the estimate) |
| **Realistic must-haves:** a clear essential and desirable split; essentials few (3 to 5 outside UN grade formulas); none inflated for the level; the real essentials stated (local language, licence, registration) | 15 | long undifferentiated list, or inflation for the level | short, split, level-appropriate, real essentials named | coded, with the inflation rules of Phase 3 |
| **Clear outcomes:** what the post is for and how success is seen (scope: team, budget, caseload; deliverables; results or targets) | 10 | none | scope plus measurable results | coded |
| **Field realism** (field posts only; otherwise not scored and its weight shared out): accompanied or not, hardship and R&R, housing, security, travel mode, connectivity, what is not provided | 10 | none of the conditions that apply | every applicable condition stated, or "not stated" said plainly | coded from the fact list |
| **Fairness:** no unexplained explicit restriction; a genuine requirement explained; concrete inclusion (encouragement where it matters, adjustments offered); trust (no-fee notice, official channel, reference); a low-bandwidth way to apply | 10 | an unexplained explicit restriction (caps the dimension at 1), no trust signal | no unexplained restriction, concrete inclusion, trust signals | coded; restrictions detected by rule, then confirmed |
| **Readability:** median sentence 13 to 20 words; acronyms spelt out; no catch-all lines, duplicated text, template residue, title and body mismatch or conflicting dates | 10 | several of these faults | none | automatic measures plus coded faults; French judged by sentence and word length, not an English grade formula |
| **Right length for the organisation type:** role-specific words within the format's ceiling; organisation and standard text not crowding out the role | 5 | far outside the band, or boilerplate over half the advert | within the band, boilerplate at or below the type's norm | automatic, against the style study's bands, which are ceilings, never targets, and "to test, not settled" (`employer-generator/plan-draft-v3.md`, A2): whole adverts for local NGO field posts 200 to 400 words and officer posts 350 to 600; role content for the INGO role profile 650 to 1,150 and the UN agency format 700 to 1,300; the other formats' bands set from Phase 2's own counts and recorded in the guide's next version |
| **Findability in search:** a recognised job title with function and level (not an internal code); the facts Google's job-posting data needs (title, description, posting date, closing date, organisation, place or remote, employment type, salary); no capitals or symbols in the title | 5 | internal or vague title, required facts missing | clear title, every required fact | automatic |

Each dimension's full anchors (what earns 1 and 2) are written in the Phase 0 pull request with worked examples from AidJobs' own sample adverts, not real ones.

**Calibration and reliability.** The guide is version 1 when this plan is approved. The agent recommends one recorded calibration: score a pilot of 20 adverts, adjust only the anchors that two coders read differently, record each change and its reason, rescore the 20, and freeze version 1.1 before Phase 2's main sample (the user's decision, section 15). After that, any change is a new version with every advert rescored. Two coders, independent read-only agents from different model families, score 15% of adverts blind; the target on each dimension is at least 70% exact agreement and a weighted kappa of at least 0.6 (on a 0 to 3 scale, scores agree within one point 62.5% of the time by chance, so "within one point" would prove little). A dimension below it is clarified in a new version. Optionally, the user scores 10 adverts (about 30 minutes), the survey's only human check of the guide (section 15).

**Guarding against "teaching to the test".** Scoring guides reward what they count, and templates written with the guide in view could score well without being better. Phase 5 therefore adds a forced-choice ranking ("which advert tells you more about the job and whether you should apply?") by a separate agent that never sees the guide, and a template must pass both; and no scorer or ranker is of the same model family as whoever wrote the output it judges.

**From titles to roles.** A new script, `tools/survey/roles.py`, with tests and a CI step:
- `titles` (local): reads the saved feeds and pages and writes one row per job title seen, contact details removed, to a local file in `tools/survey/raw/roles/`, never committed. Plain pages use the job links `confirm.py` already recognises; link texts that are not titles ("Apply now", "Read more", "View job") are dropped by a fixed list.
- `group`: normalises each title by fixed rules (case, punctuation, grades, reference codes and places taken out into their own fields; abbreviations expanded: "M&E" and "MEAL", "Log", "Fin", "WASH") and assigns it, by a rule table plus recorded decisions:
  - a **function** (what the person does): programme or project management; MEAL and information management; finance; grants, partnerships and fundraising; supply chain (logistics, procurement, fleet, warehouse); HR; administration; ICT; safety and security; communications and advocacy; policy and research; technical adviser; humanitarian coordination; leadership (country director, head of office); medical and clinical; engineering; community and field roles (mobiliser, enumerator, field officer); drivers and support services; care and support work (domestic charities). This draft list is the agent's, built on ReliefWeb's career categories and the UN's job networks and families (both Unverified until Phase 1 reads them); Phase 1 corrects it;
  - a **sector or theme**, where the title names one: WASH; health; nutrition; food security and livelihoods; cash and markets; protection, child protection, GBV; education; shelter and camp management; climate and environment; gender; governance, peace and human rights; mine action; agriculture; migration; disaster risk reduction; ESG and safeguards; none;
  - a **level**: assistant; officer; coordinator or manager; head or adviser; plus intern or fellow. The level is read from the title's words first, and a published grade is kept as published. The agent's proposed reading of grades, where a title has no level word: G grades as assistant; P-1, P-2 and NO-A as officer; P-3, P-4, NO-B and NO-C as coordinator or manager; P-5, D grades, NO-D and NO-E as head or adviser. Each row also records the public site's level (`apps/frontend/lib/site/facts.ts`: Entry, Mid-career, Senior, Leadership, Support staff), whose grade boundaries differ (P-4 is Senior there); the title-facts module (W-42) is where the two vocabularies are reconciled, and this survey only offers its counts as evidence;
  - **contexts**, each one of two values or "unknown": field or headquarters; humanitarian or development; national or international; staff or consultant. Titles seldom show them; the listing's place, the organisation type and words such as "national", "emergency" and "consultant" set them where they can, and Phase 2 fills the rest.
- Titles the rules cannot place go to a review list; the agent decides each with its reason, as in steps D to F. A decision is committed (`role-decisions.csv`) with its normalised title only when that title is seen at two or more organisations; otherwise it stays local with the titles and is counted in the committed summary as decided by hand.
- `counts` (local): writes the committed files: for each organisation and snapshot, its count of titles by function, sector, level and context (`role-counts.csv`, no titles), and the title variants pooled across organisations, each seen at two or more of them, with no organisation named (`title-variants.csv`).
- `build`: writes the role map from those committed files and the decisions. CI reruns `build` and the grouping rules' tests (on fixtures written by AidJobs) and fails on any difference.

**A role family** is a function, split by sector only where the content differs: Phase 1 records every function and sector pair with its count; Phase 3 decides which pairs need their own template (for example a WASH programme manager's duties differ from a generic programme manager's) and which share the function's core with a sector module. That keeps the library small enough to be excellent: a function core, sector modules, and level and context variants, rather than hundreds of separate templates.

**Choosing wave 1's roles.** Coverage counts each organisation's titles once per snapshot, capped at 20, shared within each organisation type. Value to paying employers weights every type outside the UN system (NGOs and INGOs, Red Cross and Red Crescent, donors and government, contractors, foundations, research bodies, domestic charities) at 1 and the UN system at 0.5. The weights are the agent's proposal, for the user's decision, from the user's view of 5 November 2026 that the UN hardly pays to publish jobs. The agent ranks families by that weighted coverage, takes the smallest set that covers most of it while appearing in at least three types, and brings the list of 10 to 12 to the user with the next 10 and the reasons; the user decides. An Estimate from the style study and the demo, not used for the selection: programme management, MEAL, finance, supply chain, HR, administration, WASH, health, protection, education, food security and livelihoods, and communications are likely.

**Output.** The role map: for each family, its count and share per organisation type, by level and context where known, with its title variants, the source of every count, and the biases above. It also gives the title-facts module (W-42) evidence for its fixed lists of functions and levels, and the landing pages and keyword families (growth plan 4.5) their job-title variants, without changing either step's plan.

## 7. Phase 2: structure by role and organisation type

**Sample.** Wave 1 reads about 350 adverts: for each chosen family, about 3 adverts in every organisation type where the role is advertised (up to 5 in the largest cells), spread across levels and contexts, and about a quarter in French (section 11). The sample is drawn by script with a fixed random seed from Phase 1's job links; a page that is refused, gone or a duplicate (the same advert reposted) is replaced by the next in the random order, and every replacement recorded. The style study's 40 adverts are not counted unless re-read under the guide.

**Where the adverts come from, per organisation type.** Phase 1's job links will not cover every type: step F's readable listings are mostly plain pages and the Workable, Greenhouse and similar feeds, 61 first-batch sites build their listings with scripts, and Workday's 20 employers cannot be listed without a POST. Donors and government, contractors, development banks, UN agencies and Red Cross bodies may have few links. After Phase 1, the agent reports a table per type of the links available and the fallback for each shortfall, for the user's approval with the reading batch. The first sources are already on hand or a decision away (section 17): the 116 full adverts in step F's saved feeds, less the AI test set (section 10), and Common Crawl's archived job pages if decision 14 allows them. The fallbacks, in order: the job's own page found through search results only (as in steps E and F), then read with a single GET, never a POST; AidJobs' own stored descriptions (decision 5, section 15); a mirror for refused UN pages (section 3). A type that still falls short is recorded as a gap with its count, not filled from elsewhere.

**Reading.** One request per advert to the employer's own job page (about 350), plus `robots.txt` for each new host (about 150), in two or three approved batches of about 200, the same host at most 15 adverts per batch. AidJobs' own stored descriptions (ICRC and Oxfam GB, once W-37's capture is merged and crawled) need no request, and are used for this analysis only if the user approves it under growth plan decision 5, attribution and the opt-out (decision 5, section 15). Each page is saved locally, cleaned to plain text (contacts removed), and kept out of git.

**Recorded per advert** (`adverts.csv`, `advert-sections.csv`):
- organisation, type, family, level, contexts, language, how it was read (own site, mirror, AidJobs' database), date read;
- the sections in order, each mapped to a fixed list of section types (header facts, about the organisation, context, role purpose, scope, responsibilities, deliverables or outcomes, working relationships, essential requirements, desirable requirements, competencies or behaviours, languages, field conditions, pay and benefits, commitments and safeguarding, equal opportunity and inclusion, how to apply, selection process, no-fee and fraud notice, data protection, other standard text), with words and bullets per section;
- total words and role-specific words;
- **the share of standard text**, by script: a sentence that recurs in two or more of the same organisation's adverts, or in a list of known sector standard texts (the UN Considerations, for example), counts as standard; where an organisation has one advert in the sample, the coder estimates it, marked Estimate;
- **facts given and missing**, from a fixed list of about 30 (pay and its form, grade or band, contract type and length, hours or level of effort, duty station and remote, travel or roving share, reports to, direct reports, budget held, closing date, its time and time zone, start date, languages and levels, selection steps, application method and documents, no-fee notice, safeguarding level or checks, accompanied or not, hardship and R&R, housing and what is provided, number of posts, funding status (proposal or contingent), equal-opportunity or inclusion statement, adjustments offered, explicit restrictions by category, reference number);
- the score on each dimension, the coder, and the guide's version.

## 8. Phase 3: content, the core

**Units.** Each section is broken into units by a read-only research agent working on the saved plain text, into a fixed schema (`units.csv`):
- **duties:** the action (a normalised verb from a fixed list), the object (a category from each role's codebook, grown during coding and recorded), the specifics given (number, place, named standard, tool or system, frequency, counterpart, scale, deadline), a specificity score from 0 to 3, and the agent's paraphrase of at most 12 words;
- **requirements:** the type (education, years of experience, technical skill, tool, language and level, licence or registration, context experience, behaviour, certification), essential or desirable, and whether it is inflated for the level, with the rule that says so;
- **facts, conditions, outcomes and statements** in their own kinds, so the omission analysis can count them.

**Inflation rules,** fixed before coding: years of experience against the level (and, for UN posts, the grade's own minimum, which is not inflation); a postgraduate degree for assistant and officer posts outside the UN's grade formulas; senior skills for junior or short posts (grant management for a 30-day surge post, donor regulations for a mobiliser; both seen in the style study); a requirement with no matching duty.

**Reliability.** A second agent, of another model family, codes 15% of adverts blind; agreement is reported per field (exact agreement, and a weighted kappa for the specificity score), and a field below 70% exact agreement or a kappa of 0.6 is clarified in the codebook and recoded.

**Comparisons,** each reported with its counts (never as statistical significance: the cells are small): across organisation types, levels and contexts, the mix of unit kinds, specificity, the most common actions and objects, requirement inflation, and words by section; and, within each family, what the top quarter of adverts contains that the bottom quarter lacks. For that comparison, adverts are ranked by their total score without the specificity dimension, so the facts found are not ones the ranking already rewarded.

**Signature facts.** For each family and level, the 3 to 6 facts that make its advert specific and that a candidate needs (for example fleet size, warehouses and procurement thresholds for logistics; the donors and their rules, budget size and accounting system for finance; sites, population served and response phase for WASH; caseload and case-management system for protection). Each is kept only if it appears more often in the top quarter than the bottom, or a normative source requires it. The chat asks for these instead of writing filler; each is recorded with the question the chat asks, in English and French, the section it feeds, and why it matters to candidates.

**Measured from the same records, for the library (section 9):** for each family, level, organisation type and context, how often each duty (action and object) appears; the years of experience asked, by level and organisation type (median and quartiles); the sections present, their order, and words per section, as the median and the best quarter's median; and the words that tell a family apart from similar ones (for example "safeguards", environmental and social standards, against "safeguarding", the protection of children and adults at risk), from the titles and duties that separate them.

**What adverts leave out:** per family, the facts and conditions most often missing, by type, with counts.

**Normative sources,** read for what each expects of each role (no text copied beyond short standard phrases), each with its publisher, edition, address, licence and reuse terms checked before it is downloaded (`normative-sources.csv`). The list (Unverified until read): the UN's generic job profiles and its values and behaviours framework; the Core Humanitarian Competency Framework; the Sphere Handbook; the Core Humanitarian Standard; the Child Protection Minimum Standards (CPMS); the INEE Minimum Standards; the IASC GBV guidelines; donor rules for finance and grants roles (for example the EU's and DG ECHO's, FCDO's, the US federal grant rules, the Global Fund's); professional bodies' profiles (CIPS for procurement, CIPD for HR, ACCA for finance); and public role profiles and curricula (NRC, IFRC, RedR, the Humanitarian Leadership Academy). About 15 to 20 documents in wave 1, in one or two approved batches; those behind a sign-in or a paywall are not read.

## 9. Phase 4: the role library and its templates

**The role library is Phase 4's main output** (the user, 10 November 2026): one machine-readable record per role family and level, with variants by organisation type and context, which the AI chat reads. Its parts:

1. **Recognition:** title variants and search terms in English and French, and the words that tell similar roles apart (for example "safeguards" against "safeguarding"; "MEAL" against "monitoring" in an engineering title), so the chat recognises the role from one line and asks only when two families stay close. The same variants and terms serve findability in search (growth plan 4.5).
2. **Signature facts:** the 3 to 6 facts the chat asks for before writing (fleet size and the procurement threshold for logistics; the donors and their rules for finance; sites, population and phase for WASH), each with its question in English and French, the section it feeds, and why candidates need it.
3. **Duty bank:** the role's usual function groups; for each duty, the action, the object and the slots for specifics (`Manage a fleet of [number] vehicles across [number] sites`); how often it appears in the adverts read, by level and context; one AidJobs-written phrasing at the best quarter's standard; and the weak versions to avoid, described in AidJobs' words as patterns (for example a catch-all "liaise with stakeholders" with no object, counterpart or frequency), or quoted only as standard phrases under 15 words, marked *(std)*.
4. **Requirements by level:** essential and desirable items; the years of experience measured by level and organisation type; and the point above which a requirement counts as inflated (the inflation rules of section 8, with the measured upper quartile for that level and type), with the prompt that questions it ("Is a master's degree truly needed for this officer post?") and the real essentials it should not drop (a local language, a licence, a professional registration).
5. **Standards, frameworks and tools** per role (for example Sphere, CPMS, CHS, Kobo), with when each applies; and the competency or behaviour framework per organisation type (for example the UN's values and behaviours framework, the Core Humanitarian Competency Framework).
6. **What adverts for the role usually leave out,** with how often, as the chat's checklist.
7. **Measured structure and length** per organisation type and role: the sections present, their order, and words per section (the median and the best quarter's median). These replace the length ranges in the AI's writing guides, which today come from the 40-advert style study (`lib/employerDemoAi/guides.ts` on `demo/employer`, uncommitted work in progress when read on 10 November 2026), when the demo or the generator adopts the library (section 16). The scoring guide's length dimension (section 5) moves to the measured bands in its next version after Phase 2, and every advert is rescored then.
8. **One reference job description per main role and format,** at the family's most common level, written by the agent for AidJobs (not by the Phase 5 models) and kept only when the blind scorer places it in the best quarter and it passes the forced-choice ranking (section 10), in English and in French. They serve as the AI's worked examples (in place of the demo's provisional `examples.ts`, when the demo or the generator adopts the library) and as the standard for tests.
9. **The rest of the template, as before:** results at 3, 6 and 12 months and a typical week for field posts, offered only as suggestions the employer confirms (the style study ranks outcome statements low for driver, nurse or enumerator posts and warns that they invite invention); field-context defaults, the conditions block's questions for field posts; assessment suggestions by level (a written test, a practical task, a structured interview); fairness notes, the genuine requirements this role sometimes has (female staff for some protection and health posts, for example), each tied to the fairness check's reason list (`employer-side-plan.md`, section 20.2); the salary basis, where pay for the role is usually stated and the safe forms to offer (a range, a named scale, a "from" figure, allowances), never a figure AidJobs invents.

**Evidence on every line:** the number of adverts it rests on, their organisation types, the source records (unit, advert and normative-source ids), and its label (Fact, Estimate, Unverified). Every line meant to be shown to employers has English and French text.

**Pooling and thresholds (the agent's choice, decision 17).** Wave 1 reads about 3 adverts per family and organisation type, so most cells are small. Measures are therefore pooled where they can be: structure and length per organisation type across all families (about 30 adverts per type), and role content per family across all types (about 30 adverts per family). A finer value (per family and type, per family and level, or per family, level and type) is stored only where its cell meets one threshold used throughout: at least 5 adverts for a median, a frequency or a variant, and at least 8 for a best-quarter figure (as section 5's rule). Below it, the line inherits the pooled value and says so, and the cell is marked thin, never filled from another type. Most wave-1 variants by organisation type will be thin; later waves fill them.

**How it is built.** A script, `tools/survey/library.py`, builds the library from the cleaned records, with tests. Measured parts are computed by the script from the coded records (frequencies, years, sections and lengths); written parts are copied from the coded files that hold them, each line keeping its evidence. The inputs: `role-map.csv`, `role-families.csv`, `title-variants.csv` and `role-distinctions.csv` (part 1); `signature-facts.csv` (part 2); `units.csv` and `template-lines.csv` (parts 3, 4 and 9); `normative-sources.csv` (part 5); `adverts.csv` (part 6, and the held-out flag); `advert-sections.csv` (part 7); `validation.csv` and the reference job descriptions (part 8). It writes one JSON file per family and an index under `docs/architecture/role-survey/library/`, and a JSON Schema beside them. Every file carries `schema_version` as `major.minor.patch` (for example `1.0.0`): the major number changes when a field is removed, renamed, changes type or meaning, or becomes required; the minor when an optional field is added; the patch for a fix to the schema's descriptions with no change to its structure. A reader refuses an unknown major version, and accepts a higher minor version, ignoring the fields it does not know. Each file also carries `built_from` (the commit and date of the records it was built from), so a change of content shows without a change of schema. CI rebuilds the library from the committed records, fails on any difference, and checks every file against the schema with the script's own check of a stated subset of JSON Schema (`type`, `required`, `enum`, `items`, `additionalProperties: false`), tested; `jsonschema` as a test-only dependency is the alternative, for the user's decision. The schema has an independent review before its first version is committed, because the generator will depend on it. Where the library lives later (a database table read by the job-description generator) is decided with the generator; until then it is files with a schema (the user, 10 November 2026).

**How a template meets a format.** A JD format (the organisation type's structure and voice, already in the demo) decides the sections and how they are phrased; the role library supplies the content in that style, and its measured structure sets the length. The organisation's own job profiles, once it has them (`employer-side-plan.md`, section 19, step 10), come before AidJobs' library.

**The template lines** (`template-lines.csv`) are the coded source of parts 3, 4 and 9 (part 2's questions are in `signature-facts.csv`); all their wording is AidJobs' own, and one readable file per family under `role-survey/templates/` renders them for people.

## 10. Phase 5: validation

- **Briefs from real adverts.** For each wave-1 family, three briefs, each built only from the facts one real advert of the family's median or bottom quarter states (for example a national NGO field post, an INGO country-office post, and the family's most common other type), so a template cannot score well merely because its brief is complete. The template's output for each brief is compared with that source advert and with the family's top quarter. A second run adds the answers to the template's signature-fact questions, taken from a top-quarter advert of the same family, to show what asking for them adds; it is reported, not used to pass.
- **Blind scoring.** An independent read-only agent, which has not seen the templates, receives real adverts from every quartile and the template outputs, all in the same plain layout without branding, with standard text set aside as section 5 describes (decision facts and fairness scored from the full text), and scores each with the guide. A separate agent, which never sees the guide, ranks the same set by forced choice. Neither is of the same model family as whoever wrote the outputs. A template passes only on both measures and every brief (section 5); one that fails is revised and tested again by new agents. ("The blind scorer" elsewhere in this plan means this agent.)
- **Template outputs.** First assembled by hand from the library for each brief, which tests the content. Then written by the models the user chose for Phase 5, Claude Sonnet 5.5 and GPT-6.1 Terra through OpenRouter (the user, 10 November 2026; they replace Kimi K2.5 and Gemma 4 here), with the library as grounding, in a model test round with its own approval (decision 16), which tests the AI and its fact checks.
- **What goes to OpenRouter.** Only AidJobs' own text: the briefs (facts, and units in AidJobs' words of at most 12 words, which pass the 8-word copy check of section 3), with the organisation shown only by its type; the library; the instructions. Never an advert's text. The round uses OpenRouter's settings that route only to providers that neither train on nor retain the data, with OpenRouter's and the providers' terms recorded before it runs. The blind scorers, which read real adverts, run as read-only agents in Cursor, as the reviews do, not through OpenRouter.
- **Fact checks.** The checks the demo's automatic checker makes (`lib/employerDemoAi/quality.ts` on `demo/employer`, uncommitted work in progress when read on 10 November 2026; adopted from it once committed): numbers not given, claims about the post nobody made, filler, raised education, labels left in English in another language, and the facts the user gave. Added for this test: every fact of the brief present; every slot filled from the brief or left as a visible placeholder; no date, amount, link or contact the brief did not give.
- **The AI test set: what a brief holds** (the user, 10 November 2026). For 20 to 30 of the 116 full adverts on disk (section 17), the facts only: title, organisation type, level, context, place, contract and its length, pay as stated, closing date, languages, reporting line, team and budget, and the duties and requirements as units in AidJobs' own words of at most 12 words. No copied text. An AI draft written from a brief is checked automatically against that advert's facts, and scored blind against the guide beside the real advert. The set checks faithfulness to facts: its briefs are complete, so it is not the pass rule for templates (the median and bottom-quarter briefs above are).
- **How the set is drawn and held out (the agent's choice, decision 17).** By script with a fixed seed, in this order: first Phase 0's pilot of 20 (at most 10 from any organisation), then the test set from the rest, with at most 10 from IMPACT Initiatives (63 on disk), 10 from World Relief (39), 5 from CDP (11) and 1 from Technopolis (3), so 20 to 26 adverts. Its adverts are flagged `held out` in `adverts.csv` and never used as evidence for the library, so the test does not grade the library on its own sources; the same organisations' other adverts stay in the evidence, so results are also reported per organisation. It is narrow, four organisations, and is widened toward 30 with adverts from Common Crawl's archive as Phase 2 reads them, each such advert held out of Phase 2's evidence too.
- **Where the briefs are kept** (decision 15). If decision 15 takes the recommended option, the briefs stay local and git-ignored in `tools/survey/raw/ai-test-set/` (each comes from one employer's advert, and the employer survey's rule commits no page content); their schema, a checksum per brief and the results by brief are committed (`role-survey/ai-test-set/`).
- **The reference job descriptions** (section 9, part 8) go through the same blind scoring: a reference is kept only when it scores in its family's best quarter and passes the forced-choice ranking; then it becomes the AI's worked example and the standard the tests compare drafts with.
- **Who scores.** The blind scorers and the forced-choice ranker come from a model family different from every writer in the set: Claude and GPT, and the agent that assembled outputs or wrote the references.
- **Real use, later.** Once the generator is live, how often employers keep, edit or delete each template line (counts per line only, no text stored), with a threshold of use before a line loses the label.
- **The label.** Until then the templates are shown as "based on evidence, not yet tested with hiring managers". The limit is stated plainly: no hiring manager or practitioner has reviewed them, and blind scoring by an agent measures the guide's criteria, not hiring outcomes.
